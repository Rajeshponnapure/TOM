## 1. Cold Email Templates

### The Classic Outreach

```
Subject: [Mutual Connection / Specific Reference] — [Value Proposition]

Hi [First Name],

[Personalized opening — reference their work, company news, or a mutual connection]

I'm reaching out because [specific reason directly related to them].

[Value proposition in 1-2 sentences — what's in it for them?]

Would you be open to a [15-minute call / quick chat] this [week / next week] to discuss?

Best,
[Your Name]
[Your Title]
[Company]
[Link to your LinkedIn / portfolio]
```

### The Compliment Approach

```
Subject: Loved your talk on [Topic]

Hi [First Name],

I watched your presentation on [Topic] at [Conference / Video] — specifically
the part about [specific detail]. Your approach to [concept] was insightful,
especially how you [specific technique].

I've been working on a similar challenge at [Your Company], and we found
that [relevant insight]. Would love to pick your brain for 10 minutes.

Available [Day] or [Day]?

Best,
[Your Name]
```

### The Value-First Pitch

```
Subject: Quick idea for [Their Company]

Hi [First Name],

I analyzed [Their Company]'s [specific page / metric / product] and noticed
that [specific observation — show you did your homework].

We helped [Similar Company] achieve [specific result] by [specific approach].
I think we could do something similar for you.

Worth a quick call?

Best,
[Your Name]
```

## 2. Follow-Up Sequences

### Sequence Strategy

```
Email 1: Initial outreach (Day 0) — Value proposition
Email 2: Follow-up (Day 3) — Additional value / case study
Email 3: Breakup (Day 7) — Direct ask + open loop
Email 4: Final (Day 14) — Polite close, leave door open
```

### Follow-Up Templates

```
Subject: Re: [Original Subject]

Hi [First Name],

Following up on my email from [Day of week]. I know you're busy,
but I'd love to get your thoughts on [specific point].

Here's a [case study / resource / article] that might be relevant:
[Link]

Would [Day] or [Day] work for a quick call?

Best,
[Your Name]
```

```
Subject: [Original Subject] + case study

Hi [First Name],

One more thing — we recently helped [Company Name] achieve [specific result].
Here's the full story: [Link]

Thought this might be useful as you consider [their use case].

No pressure, but I'd love to chat if this resonates.

Best,
[Your Name]
```

```
Subject: Closing the loop on [Topic]

Hi [First Name],

I haven't heard back, so I'll assume now isn't a good time.

If things change, feel free to grab time on my calendar here: [Calendly Link]

Wishing you a great [season / month]!

Best,
[Your Name]
```

## 3. Formal vs Semi-Formal Tone

### Formal Tone (Government, Legal, Academia, C-Suite)

```
Dear [Title] [Last Name],

I hope this message finds you well.

I am writing to formally request [specific item]. Please find attached
the relevant documentation for your review.

Should you require any additional information, please do not hesitate
to contact me.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

Respectfully,
[Your Full Name]
[Title]
[Contact Information]
```

### Semi-Formal (Most Business, Startups, Mid-Management)

```
Hi [First Name],

Hope you're having a good week.

Just following up on our conversation about [topic]. I've attached
the proposal for your review.

Let me know if you have any questions!

Best,
[Your Name]
```

### Tone Cheat Sheet

| Context | Greeting | Sign-off |
|---------|----------|----------|
| First contact with unknown | Dear [Title] [Last Name] | Respectfully / Sincerely |
| Existing business contact | Hi [First Name] | Best / Regards |
| Internal team | Hey [Name] / Morning | Thanks / Cheers |
| Close colleague | Hey / Hello | Thanks / See you |
| Client — formal | Dear [Full Name] | Best regards |
| Client — established | Hi [First Name] | Best / Thanks |
| Investor / Board | Dear [Title] [Last Name] | Sincerely / Warmly |
| Vendor / Partner | Hi [First Name] | Best / Looking forward |

## 4. Subject Line Optimization

### Formulas That Work

| Formula | Example | Open Rate Impact |
|---------|---------|-----------------|
| [Number] [Adjective] ways to [Result] | "3 Simple ways to double your open rates" | +30% |
| Question format | "Are you making this SEO mistake?" | +25% |
| Curiosity gap | "The one email template that works every time" | +40% |
| Personalization | "[Name], quick question about [Topic]" | +50% |
| Urgency (use sparingly) | "Last chance: [Offer] ends Friday" | +20% |
| How-to | "How [Company] reduced costs by 40%" | +35% |
| Breaking news | "New update: [Product] just launched [Feature]" | +20% |

### Subject Line Rules

- **Keep under 50 characters** (mobile truncation at ~60 chars)
- **No ALL CAPS** or excessive punctuation (!!?)
- **Avoid spam words**: free, guarantee, act now, limited time
- **Personalize**: include recipient name or company
- **Test emojis** — can boost open rates 10-15% in casual contexts
- **Re:** prefix for follow-ups (looks like continuation)
- **A/B test** subject lines on lists > 1,000

## 5. Call-to-Action Placement

### CTA Best Practices

```
Email structure with CTA placement:

| Opening (2-3 lines) | → Context, personalization |
| Body (3-5 lines)    | → Value proposition, evidence |
| CTA (1 line)        | → Single, clear, specific ask |
| Closing (1-2 lines) | → Sign-off, PS optional |
```

### CTA Examples

```
Weak: "Let me know if you're interested."
Strong: "Are you free Tuesday at 2 PM for a 15-minute call?"

Weak: "Check out our website."
Strong: "Here's the direct link to the case study: [Link]"

Weak: "I hope to hear from you soon."
Strong: "If this sounds interesting, reply 'Yes' and I'll send over the details."

Weak: "Let's connect sometime."
Strong: "I'll be in [City] on [Date]. Coffee that week?"
```

### CTA Rules

- **One CTA per email** — multiple choices reduce conversion
- **Make it easy to say yes** — low commitment first
- **Specify timeline** — "this week" vs "sometime"
- **Remove friction** — include calendar link, phone number
- **Use action verbs** — grab, join, see, try, schedule

## 6. Email Structure

### The Perfect Email Formula

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Subject: [Compelling, clear subject]   │
├─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Hi [First Name],                       │
│                                       │
│ [Opening — context + personalization]  │
│                                       │
│ [Body — value proposition]             │
│                                       │
│ [Evidence — social proof / data]       │
│                                       │
│ [CTA — single, specific ask]          │
│                                       │
│ Best,                                 │
│ [Your Name]                           │
│ [Title]                               │
│ [Company]                             │
│ [Phone / Website]                     │
├─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ P.S. [Optional — emphasis or extra] │
└─────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Opening Lines

```
Good: "I came across your article on [Topic] and thought your perspective on [Detail] was spot on."
Bad: "I hope this email finds you well."

Good: "[Mutual Connection] suggested I reach out about [Topic]."
Bad: "I'm writing to you today because..."

Good: "I noticed that [Their Company] recently [News / Launch / Hire]."
Bad: "My name is [Name] and I work at [Company]."
```

### Closing Lines

```
Action-oriented: "Would [Day] or [Day] work for a 15-minute call?"
Question-oriented: "Is this something you'd be open to discussing?"
Value-oriented: "I'd love to share how we helped [Similar Company] achieve [Result]."
Soft close: "Let me know if this is worth a deeper conversation."
```

## 7. Email Length Optimization

### Length Guidelines

| Context | Optimal Length | Reason |
|---------|---------------|--------|
| Cold email | 50-125 words | Skim-friendly |
| Follow-up | 30-75 words | Quick reminder |
| Internal update | 100-200 words | Brief context |
| Proposal | 200-400 words | Include details |
| Client communication | 100-250 words | Clear but thorough |
| Thank you | 25-50 words | Quick appreciation |

### Skimmability

- **Bullet points** for lists (3-5 items max)
- **Bold** key phrases for scanners
- **Short paragraphs** (2-3 sentences max)
- **White space** between sections
- **Numbers** stand out visually

## 8. Personalization Techniques

### Research Sources

| Data Point | Source |
|------------|--------|
| Recent company news | Google News, Crunchbase |
| Personal achievement | LinkedIn, Twitter |
| Published content | Medium, personal blog |
| Conference talks | YouTube, conference site |
| Mutual connections | LinkedIn |
| Company metrics | BuiltWith, SimilarWeb |
| Tech stack | StackShare, Wappalyzer |

### Personalization Levels

```
Level 1: {First Name} only — basic, detectable
Level 2: + Company name — slightly better
Level 3: + Role/Industry — shows some research
Level 4: + Recent article/post — shows real effort
Level 5: + Specific insight on their work — impressive
Level 6: + Mutual connection reference — highest conversion
```

### Intelligent Personalization

```
Bad: "Hi Tom, I see you work at [Company]. We can help [Company] grow."
Good: "Hi Tom, noticed you recently published about [Specific Topic] — your take on [Detail] was refreshing."
Best: "Hi Tom, [Mutual Contact] mentioned you're working on [Specific Project]. We tackled a similar challenge at [Past Company] by [Approach]."
```

## 9. A/B Testing Subject Lines

### Test Variables

| Variable | Test Example A | Test Example B |
|----------|---------------|---------------|
| Length | "New feature launch" | "Introducing [Feature]: Save 10 hours/week" |
| Personalization | "Quick question" | "Tom, quick question about [Topic]" |
| Question vs statement | "Interested?" | "Here's how it works" |
| Curiosity vs clarity | "The secret to..." | "How we increased X by Y%" |
| Emoji vs no emoji | "New update 🚀" | "New update" |
| Urgency vs value | "Last chance to..." | "See what you're missing" |

### Testing Methodology

```
1. Split list randomly (50/50)
2. Run for 24-48 hours minimum
3. Measure: open rate, reply rate, click rate
4. Use statistical significance (p < 0.05)
5. Document winners for future reference
```

## 10. Email Tracking Etiquette

### What to Track

| Metric | What It Tells You | Action |
|--------|------------------|--------|
| Open rate | Subject line effectiveness | Optimize subject lines |
| Click rate | CTA effectiveness | Test different CTAs |
| Reply rate | Message resonance | Refine value prop |
| Bounce rate | List quality | Clean your list |
| Unsubscribe rate | Relevance | Segment better |

### Tracking Ethics

- **Disclose tracking** if in EU (GDPR) or California (CCPA)
- **Don't mention opens** in follow-ups (creepy)
- **Use pixel tracking** responsibly
- **Track links** for click data, not pixel
- **Don't abuse** open data to pressure recipients

## 11. Attachment Best Practices

### Rules

- **Don't attach in cold emails** — link to a shared drive or website
- **PDF preferred over Word/Excel** — universal viewing
- **Use descriptive filenames**: `Q2-2026-Report-Tom-Co.pdf` not `document.pdf`
- **Compress large files** — keep under 5MB
- **Reference attachments in body**: "I've attached the proposal as outlined"
- **Password protect** sensitive documents with separate notification

### Alternative to Attachments

```
Instead of attaching:
  "Please find attached the proposal."

Say:
  "Here's a link to the proposal: [Link]. It only takes 2 minutes to review."
```

## 12. International Email Etiquette

| Culture | Formality Level | Greeting | Sign-off |
|---------|----------------|----------|---------|
| USA | Moderate | Hi / Hello | Best / Thanks |
| UK | High | Dear | Kind regards |
| Germany | Very High | Dear [Title] [Last Name] | Mit freundlichen Grüßen |
| Japan | Very High | 件名: / 様 | 敬具 / よろしくお願いします |
| France | High | Madame / Monsieur | Cordialement / Bien à vous |
| Brazil | Low-Moderate | Olá / Prezado | Abraços / Atenciosamente |
| India | Moderate | Dear / Respected | Regards / Thanks |
| Scandinavia | Low | Hi / Hej | Best / Mvh |

### Cross-Cultural Tips

- **Avoid idioms** ("ballpark figure", "touch base") — confusing for non-native
- **Use clear dates** — 25 May 2026 over 5/25/2026
- **Specify timezone** — "3 PM EST" not just "3 PM"
- **Be direct with Northern Europe**; add context for Asia/Latin America
- **Use simple vocabulary** — "use" not "utilize", "help" not "facilitate"
- **Avoid humour/sarcasm** — easily misinterpreted

### Essential Elements

```
[First Name] [Last Name]
[Title]
[Company]
[Phone] | [Website]
[LinkedIn URL]
```

### Signature Variations

```

Tom Anderson
Senior Developer, Acme Corp

linkedin.com/in/tomanderson

Tom Anderson
Acme Corp · tom@acme.com

Full (executive):
─────────────────
Tom Anderson
Chief Technology Officer
Acme Corp
T: +1 (555) 123-4567
E: tom@acme.com
W: acme.com
✉ Available for speaking engagements

Promotional (marketing):
─────────────────
Tom Anderson | Product Lead, Acme Corp
📧 tom@acme.com | 📞 +1 (555) 123-4567
📅 Book a demo: acme.com/demo
🎙 Listen to our podcast: acme.fm
```

### Signature Rules

- **Keep under 5 lines** (not including legal disclaimers)
- **No images** (blocked by email clients)
- **One link to LinkedIn** (not multiple social links)
- **No inspirational quotes** (distracting)
- **Legal disclaimers** separate with a line
- **Mobile-friendly** — check rendering on phone

## 14. Timing Strategies

### Best Send Times

| Day | Best Time (Recipient's Timezone) |
|-----|--------------------------------|
| Tuesday | 10:00 AM — 11:00 AM |
| Wednesday | 10:00 AM — 12:00 PM |
| Thursday | 8:00 AM — 10:00 AM |
| Monday | 11:00 AM — 1:00 PM (avoid early AM — inbox flood) |
| Friday | 8:00 AM — 9:00 AM (avoid afternoon) |
| Weekend | Avoid (unless B2C) |

### Timezone Considerations

- **Target 8-10 AM local time** for highest open rates
- **Use tools**: Mailchimp, HubSpot auto-optimize per recipient
- **Tuesday-Thursday** best days; avoid Monday AM / Friday PM
- **Holiday weeks**: Send important emails before or after

### Follow-up Timing

| Step | Delay | Notes |
|------|-------|-------|
| Initial email | Day 0 | Monday-Wednesday |
| Follow-up 1 | Day 3-4 | Thursday-Friday |
| Follow-up 2 | Day 7-10 | Next week |
| Final follow-up | Day 14-21 | Breakup email |
| Re-engage | 30-60 days | New value/campaign |

## Email Writing Checklist

### Before Sending
- [ ] Subject line < 50 characters, compelling, personal
- [ ] Greeting properly addressed (name, title)
- [ ] Opening shows research/personalization
- [ ] Body is concise (under 200 words where possible)
- [ ] One clear CTA per email
- [ ] Proofread for typos, grammar, tone
- [ ] Attachments included and referenced
- [ ] Signature complete and professional

### Sequence Setup
- [ ] 3-4 email sequence designed
- [ ] Each email provides new value
- [ ] Follow-ups spaced 3-5 days apart
- [ ] Breakup email at end
- [ ] Tracking enabled (respectfully)
- [ ] A/B test subject lines on first email
- [ ] Personalization tokens verified
