## 1. Human Anatomy & Physiology

### 1.1 Cardiovascular System

The cardiovascular system comprises the heart, blood vessels (arteries, veins, capillaries), and blood. It delivers oxygen, nutrients, hormones, and immune cells while removing metabolic wastes.

**Heart:** A four-chambered muscular pump (right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium, left ventricle) located in the mediastinum. The right side pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs (pulmonary circulation); the left side pumps oxygenated blood to the body (systemic circulation). Cardiac output = heart rate x stroke volume (normal ~4-8 L/min).

**Cardiac Conduction System:** SA node (pacemaker, 60-100 bpm) -> AV node (40-60 bpm) -> Bundle of His -> bundle branches -> Purkinje fibers. The autonomic nervous system modulates rate: sympathetic (B1-adrenergic) increases, parasympathetic (vagus, muscarinic) decreases.

**Coronary Arteries:** Left main -> LAD (anterior wall, septum) + circumflex (lateral wall); Right coronary artery (inferior wall, RV, SA/AV nodes in most people).

**Blood Vessels:** Arteries carry blood away (high pressure, thick muscular walls); arterioles regulate resistance; capillaries are exchange vessels (one-cell-thick endothelium); venules and veins return blood (low pressure, valves prevent backflow).

**Blood Components:** Plasma (55%, water, electrolytes, proteins), RBCs (erythrocytes, ~5 million/uL, hemoglobin for O2/CO2 transport), WBCs (leukocytes, ~5000-10000/uL, immune defense), platelets (thrombocytes, ~150000-400000/uL, hemostasis).

**Common Disorders:** Hypertension, coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, heart failure, arrhythmias, valvular heart disease, cardiomyopathy, peripheral artery disease, deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism, aortic aneurysm, aortic dissection, congenital heart defects.

### 1.2 Respiratory System

Comprises the upper airway (nasal cavity, pharynx, larynx) and lower respiratory tract (trachea, bronchi, bronchioles, alveoli). Primary function: gas exchange (O2 in, CO2 out).

**Upper Airway:** Nasal cavity (filters, warms, humidifies), pharynx (nasopharynx, oropharynx, laryngopharynx), larynx (voice production, epiglottis prevents aspiration).

**Lower Airway:** Trachea (C-shaped cartilages) -> right and left main bronchi -> lobar bronchi -> segmental bronchi -> bronchioles -> terminal bronchioles -> respiratory bronchioles -> alveolar ducts -> alveolar sacs.

**Alveoli:** ~300 million tiny air sacs with enormous surface area (~100 m2) for gas exchange. Type I pneumocytes (gas exchange) and Type II pneumocytes (surfactant production). Surfactant reduces surface tension, preventing alveolar collapse.

**Mechanics:** Inspiration is active (diaphragm contracts, external intercostals lift rib cage); expiration is passive at rest (elastic recoil). Tidal volume ~500 mL, minute ventilation ~6 L/min.

**Control of Breathing:** Medullary respiratory center (dorsal: inspiration; ventral: expiration), pontine centers modulate rhythm. Chemoreceptors (central: CO2/pH in CSF; peripheral: carotid/aortic bodies for O2, CO2, pH).

**Common Disorders:** Asthma, COPD (emphysema, chronic bronchitis), pneumonia, tuberculosis, lung cancer, pulmonary embolism, pulmonary fibrosis, pneumothorax, pleural effusion, COVID-19, influenza pneumonia, ARDS, bronchiolitis, cystic fibrosis.

### 1.3 Nervous System

Divided into central nervous system (brain and spinal cord) and peripheral nervous system (cranial and spinal nerves).

**Brain:** Cerebrum (cortex: frontal, parietal, temporal, occipital lobes; basal ganglia; limbic system; hippocampus; amygdala), cerebellum (coordination, balance), brainstem (midbrain, pons, medulla oblongata). ~86 billion neurons.

**Functional Regions:** Frontal lobe (executive function, motor cortex, Broca area [speech production]); Parietal lobe (somatosensory cortex, spatial awareness); Temporal lobe (auditory cortex, Wernicke area [language comprehension], memory); Occipital lobe (visual cortex); Cerebellum (coordination, fine motor); Brainstem (cardiac, respiratory centers, consciousness).

**Spinal Cord:** Extends from foramen magnum to L1-L2. 31 segments (8 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 sacral, 1 coccygeal). Central gray matter (dorsal horn: sensory; ventral horn: motor; lateral horn: autonomic). White matter tracts (dorsal columns: vibration/proprioception; spinothalamic: pain/temperature; corticospinal: voluntary motor).

**Peripheral Nervous System:** 12 pairs of cranial nerves, 31 pairs of spinal nerves. Somatic (voluntary) vs. autonomic (involuntary). Autonomic: sympathetic (fight-or-flight, thoracolumbar) and parasympathetic (rest-and-digest, craniosacral).

**Neurotransmitters:** Acetylcholine (neuromuscular junction, autonomic ganglia), dopamine (movement, reward, motivation), serotonin (mood, sleep, appetite), norepinephrine (arousal, attention), GABA (main inhibitory), glutamate (main excitatory), endorphins (pain modulation), histamine (wakefulness).

**Blood-Brain Barrier:** Tight junctions between capillary endothelial cells, supported by astrocyte foot processes. Protects brain from toxins, pathogens; regulates ion and metabolite entry.

**Common Disorders:** Stroke (ischemic, hemorrhagic), epilepsy/seizures, Parkinson disease, Alzheimer disease, multiple sclerosis, migraine, peripheral neuropathy, brain tumors (glioma, meningioma, metastasis), traumatic brain injury, spinal cord injury, meningitis, encephalitis, Guillain-Barre syndrome, ALS, Huntington disease.

### 1.4 Digestive System

Alimentary canal (mouth, pharynx, esophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, rectum, anus) plus accessory organs (salivary glands, liver, gallbladder, pancreas).

**Stomach:** J-shaped, 1-1.5 L capacity. Regions: cardia, fundus, body, antrum, pylorus. Gastric glands produce HCl (parietal cells), pepsinogen (chief cells, activated to pepsin by acid), intrinsic factor (B12 absorption), mucus. Gastrin from G cells stimulates acid secretion.

**Small Intestine:** ~6 meters, three segments: duodenum (25 cm, receives chyme, bile, pancreatic enzymes), jejunum (~2.5 m, main absorption), ileum (~3.5 m, B12 and bile acids absorption). Villi and microvilli greatly increase surface area (~200 m2).

**Large Intestine:** ~1.5 meters: cecum (appendix), colon (ascending, transverse, descending, sigmoid), rectum, anal canal. Water and electrolyte absorption, bacterial fermentation (vitamin K, biotin), feces formation.

**Liver:** Largest internal organ (~1.5 kg). Functions: metabolism (carbs, proteins, fats), bile production (~500 mL/day), detoxification, storage (glycogen, vitamins A/D/B12, iron), synthesis (albumin, clotting factors).

**Pancreas:** Exocrine (acinar cells produce digestive enzymes: trypsinogen, chymotrypsinogen, amylase, lipase; duct cells produce bicarbonate) and endocrine (islets: B-cells produce insulin, A-cells produce glucagon, D-cells produce somatostatin).

**Common Disorders:** GERD, peptic ulcer disease (H. pylori, NSAIDs), gastritis, gastroenteritis, celiac disease, IBS, Crohn disease, ulcerative colitis, diverticulitis, appendicitis, hepatitis (A, B, C, alcoholic, autoimmune), cirrhosis, pancreatitis, cholecystitis, gallstones, colorectal cancer, pancreatic cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma.

### 1.5 Endocrine System

Network of glands producing hormones that regulate metabolism, growth, reproduction, homeostasis.

**Hypothalamus-Pituitary Axis:** Hypothalamus releasing/inhibiting hormones -> anterior pituitary (GH, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, prolactin, MSH). Posterior pituitary stores/releases oxytocin and ADH.

**Thyroid:** Butterfly-shaped gland in anterior neck. Produces T4 (thyroxine) and T3 (active form). C-cells produce calcitonin. Regulated by TRH-TSH feedback loop. Increases metabolic rate, heart rate, protein synthesis, growth.

**Parathyroid Glands:** Four small glands behind thyroid. PTH increases blood Ca2+: stimulates osteoclasts, increases renal Ca2+ reabsorption, increases calcitriol production.

**Adrenal Glands:** Cortex (zona glomerulosa: aldosterone; zona fasciculata: cortisol; zona reticularis: DHEA). Medulla: epinephrine (80%) and norepinephrine (20%).

**Common Disorders:** Diabetes mellitus (type 1, type 2, gestational), thyroid disorders (hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism, Graves disease, Hashimoto thyroiditis, thyroid cancer), adrenal insufficiency (Addison disease), Cushing syndrome, hyperaldosteronism (Conn syndrome), pheochromocytoma, pituitary tumors (prolactinoma, acromegaly), PCOS, metabolic syndrome.

### 1.6 Musculoskeletal System

Comprises bones (206 in adults), muscles (>600 skeletal), joints, cartilage, tendons, ligaments.

**Bones:** Functions: support, protection, movement, mineral storage (99% of body Ca2+), hematopoiesis (red marrow). Types: long (femur), short (carpals), flat (skull), irregular (vertebrae). Remodeling: osteoblasts (formation), osteoclasts (resorption), osteocytes (maintenance).

**Muscles:** Skeletal (voluntary, striated), cardiac (involuntary, striated), smooth (involuntary, non-striated). Sliding filament theory: myosin cross-bridges pull actin filaments, shortening sarcomere. Types: Type I (slow-twitch, oxidative), Type IIa (fast-twitch, oxidative-glycolytic), Type IIb (fast-twitch, glycolytic).

**Common Disorders:** Osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, fractures (open, closed, comminuted, greenstick, stress), osteoporosis, back pain (disc herniation, spinal stenosis, sciatica), sports injuries (ACL tear, rotator cuff tear, meniscus tear, tendinitis), gout, pseudogout, septic arthritis, osteomyelitis, bone tumors.

### 1.7 Renal System

Two kidneys, two ureters, urinary bladder, urethra. Maintains fluid/electrolyte balance, acid-base balance, blood pressure, waste excretion, RBC production, vitamin D activation.

**Nephron:** Glomerulus (filtration of blood: ~180 L/day filtrate) -> PCT (reabsorption of 65% Na+, all glucose/amino acids, most HCO3-) -> Loop of Henle (countercurrent multiplier) -> DCT (fine-tuning: Ca2+ reabsorption [PTH], NaCl reabsorption [thiazide target]) -> Collecting duct (water reabsorption [ADH], Na+ reabsorption/K+ secretion [aldosterone]).

**GFR Regulation:** Autoregulation (myogenic, tubuloglomerular feedback maintains GFR ~125 mL/min), sympathetic, hormonal (RAAS, ANP).

**Common Disorders:** CKD, AKI (prerenal, renal, postrenal), glomerulonephritis (IgA nephropathy, membranous, post-streptococcal), nephrotic syndrome, nephritic syndrome, pyelonephritis, cystitis, kidney stones, renal cell carcinoma, PCKD, diabetic nephropathy.

### 1.8 Reproductive System

**Male:** Testes (spermatogenesis, testosterone), epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicles, prostate, penis. FSH -> Sertoli cells; LH -> Leydig cells.

**Female:** Ovaries (oogenesis, estrogen, progesterone), fallopian tubes, uterus, cervix, vagina, vulva. Menstrual cycle (~28 days): follicular, ovulation (day 14), luteal. Menopause ~45-55 years.

**Pregnancy:** Fertilization -> blastocyst (implantation ~6-7 days) -> embryo (weeks 3-8) -> fetus (week 9+). Gestation ~40 weeks. hCG produced by syncytiotrophoblast.

**Common Disorders (Male):** BPH, prostate cancer, testicular cancer, erectile dysfunction, infertility, varicocele, prostatitis.

**Common Disorders (Female):** Menstrual disorders, PCOS, endometriosis, uterine fibroids, ovarian cysts, cervical/ovarian/uterine cancer, PID, infertility, ectopic pregnancy, preeclampsia, miscarriage.

### 1.9 Immune System

Innate (non-specific, immediate) and adaptive (specific, memory) immunity.

**Innate Immunity:** Physical barriers (skin, mucosa), chemical (lysozyme, gastric acid, complement), phagocytes (neutrophils, macrophages, dendritic cells), NK cells, inflammation. TLRs, NLRs detect PAMPs.

**Adaptive Immunity:** B cells (bone marrow, antibodies: IgM, IgG, IgA, IgE, IgD). T cells (thymus): CD4+ helper (Th1, Th2, Th17, Treg), CD8+ cytotoxic. MHC I (all cells), MHC II (APCs).

**Hypersensitivity Reactions:** Type I (IgE: allergies, anaphylaxis); Type II (cytotoxic: autoimmune hemolytic anemia); Type III (immune complex: SLE, post-streptococcal GN); Type IV (T cell-mediated: contact dermatitis, TB skin test).

**Common Disorders:** Allergies, asthma, autoimmune diseases (RA, SLE, MS, type 1 DM, Hashimoto, Graves, celiac), immunodeficiency (SCID, HIV/AIDS, chemotherapy-induced), transplant rejection.

### 1.10 Integumentary System

Skin (largest organ, ~1.5-2 m2), hair, nails, sweat glands, sebaceous glands.

**Skin Layers:** Epidermis (keratinocytes, melanocytes, Langerhans cells, Merkel cells). Dermis (collagen, elastin, blood vessels, nerves, glands). Hypodermis (adipose tissue).

**Skin Functions:** Barrier, thermoregulation (vasodilation/constriction, sweating), sensation, vitamin D synthesis (UVB -> vitamin D3), immune surveillance, wound healing.

**Wound Healing:** Hemostasis -> Inflammation (days 1-3) -> Proliferation (granulation, angiogenesis, re-epithelialization, days 3-14) -> Remodeling (collagen maturation, scar formation).

**Common Disorders:** Acne vulgaris, eczema (atopic dermatitis), psoriasis, skin infections (cellulitis, impetigo), skin cancer (BCC, SCC, melanoma), contact dermatitis, urticaria, rosacea, vitiligo, alopecia, warts, pressure ulcers, burns.

## 2. Common Diseases & Conditions by Specialty

### 2.1 Cardiology

#### Hypertension (HTN)
**Definition:** Persistent elevated BP >=130/80 (ACC/AHA) or >=140/90 (ESC). Primary/essential (~90%) vs Secondary (~10%) from renal artery stenosis, CKD, pheochromocytoma, hyperaldosteronism, OSA, drugs.

**Pathophysiology:** Increased SVR, RAAS overactivity, Na+ retention. Target organ damage: heart (LVH, HF), brain (stroke), kidneys (CKD), eyes (retinopathy), arteries (atherosclerosis).

**Symptoms:** Often asymptomatic. Severe: occipital headache, epistaxis, visual changes.

**Diagnosis:** Multiple BP measurements. Labs: BMP, CBC, lipids, TSH, UA, ECG.

**Treatment:** Lifestyle (DASH diet, Na+ <1500 mg/day, exercise, weight loss). First-line: ACEi/ARB, CCB, thiazide. Goal <130/80 (most), <140/90 (elderly).

#### Coronary Artery Disease (CAD)
**Definition:** Atherosclerotic plaque in coronary arteries. Risk factors: HTN, dyslipidemia, DM, smoking, obesity, family history.

**Pathophysiology:** Endothelial injury -> LDL oxidation -> foam cells -> fatty streak -> plaque. Rupture -> thrombosis -> ACS.

**Stable Angina:** Chest pressure on exertion, relieved by rest/NTG. Diagnosis: ECG, stress test, CTA, angiography. Treatment: aspirin, statin (high-intensity), B-blocker, CCB, nitrates. PCI or CABG for severe.

#### Myocardial Infarction (MI)
**STEMI:** ST-elevation, complete occlusion. Emergency PCI <90 min or fibrinolytics.

**NSTEMI:** ST-depression/T inversion, +troponin. Urgent PCI for high-risk features.

**Symptoms:** Severe chest pain >20 min, dyspnea, nausea, diaphoresis. Atypical in women/elderly/diabetics.

**Diagnosis:** ECG, troponin (rises 2-4 hr, peaks 24-48 hr).

**Treatment:** MONA (Morphine, O2 if hypoxic, NTG, Aspirin). DAPT + heparin + statin + B-blocker + ACEi. PCI. Secondary prevention: BP<130/80, LDL<70, HbA1c<7%, aspirin, statin, B-blocker, cardiac rehab.

#### Heart Failure (HF)
**Definition:** Inability of heart to meet metabolic demands. HFrEF (LVEF <=40%), HFpEF (>=50%).

**Symptoms:** Dyspnea, orthopnea, PND, fatigue, edema, JVD, crackles. BNP/NT-proBNP elevated.

**Treatment (HFrEF):** B-blocker + ACEi/ARB (or ARNI) + MRA + SGLT2i. Diuretics. CRT if LBBB. ICD if LVEF <=35%. Transplant/LVAD for advanced.

#### Valvular Disease
**Aortic Stenosis:** Angina, syncope, dyspnea. Harsh murmur RUSB. Treatment: AVR once symptomatic.

**Mitral Regurgitation:** Holosystolic apical murmur. Surgery (repair preferred) for severe.

**Aortic Regurgitation:** Decrescendo diastolic murmur LSB. Wide pulse pressure. Treatment: surgery.

**Mitral Stenosis:** Rheumatic. Diastolic rumble. Treatment: rate control, diuresis, anticoagulation if AF, valvuloplasty or surgery.

#### Arrhythmias
**Atrial Fibrillation:** Most common sustained. Rate vs rhythm control. Anticoagulation per CHA2DS2-VASc.

**VT/VF:** Cardiac arrest - defibrillation. VT with pulse: amiodarone, lidocaine, cardioversion.

**Bradycardia:** Symptomatic -> atropine, transcutaneous pacing, then transvenous pacemaker.

### 2.2 Neurology

#### Stroke
**Definition:** Acute neurologic deficit from brain ischemia (87%) or hemorrhage (13%).

**Ischemic:** Thrombotic, embolic (AF, VHD), lacunar (small vessel). Hemorrhagic: ICH (HTN, amyloid, anticoagulation) or SAH (aneurysm).

**Symptoms:** FAST - Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call 911. Unilateral weakness, aphasia, visual field loss, ataxia. Hemorrhage: worst headache of life.

**Diagnosis:** Non-contrast CT (rule out hemorrhage), CTA, MRI DWI (gold for ischemia).

**Treatment:** Ischemic: IV tPA within 3-4.5 hr. Thrombectomy for large vessel occlusion up to 24 hr (advanced imaging). Aspirin at 24-48 hr. Hemorrhagic: ICU, BP control, reverse anticoagulation, manage ICP. Surgery for cerebellar ICH. Coiling/clipping for aneurysm.

#### Epilepsy/Seizures
**Definition:** Seizure = abnormal neuronal electrical activity. Epilepsy = >=2 unprovoked seizures.

**Types:** Focal (aware/impaired awareness), Generalized (tonic-clonic, absence, myoclonic, atonic).

**Diagnosis:** History, EEG, MRI brain.

**Treatment:** Focal: lamotrigine, levetiracetam, oxcarbazepine. Generalized: valproate, lamotrigine. Absence: ethosuximide. Status epilepticus: benzodiazepine -> fosphenytoin/valproate -> IV anesthetic.

#### Parkinson Disease
**Definition:** Dopaminergic neuron degeneration in substantia nigra. Lewy bodies.

**Symptoms:** TRAP: Tremor (rest, pill-rolling), Rigidity (cogwheel), Akinesia/Bradykinesia, Postural instability (late). Non-motor: anosmia, REM sleep behavior disorder, depression, autonomic dysfunction.

**Diagnosis:** Clinical. Levodopa response supports diagnosis. DaTscan differentiates.

**Treatment:** Levodopa/carbidopa (most effective). Dopamine agonists (pramipexole, ropinirole) in younger. COMT inhibitors for wearing-off. DBS for advanced.

#### Alzheimer Disease
**Definition:** Progressive dementia. Beta-amyloid plaques + tau tangles. APOE epsilon4 risk factor.

**Symptoms:** Insidious onset, short-term memory loss, word-finding difficulty, executive dysfunction, disorientation, behavioral changes.

**Diagnosis:** Clinical + MoCA. MRI: hippocampal atrophy. CSF: low Ab42, high tau. Amyloid PET.

**Treatment:** Cholinesterase inhibitors (donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine). Memantine (moderate-severe). Anti-amyloid antibodies (lecanemab, donanemab) for early.

#### Multiple Sclerosis
**Definition:** Autoimmune CNS demyelination. Women > men, onset 20-40 years.

**Types:** RRMS (85%), SPMS, PPMS (10-15%).

**Symptoms:** Optic neuritis, transverse myelitis, INO, ataxia, weakness, sensory loss, fatigue.

**Diagnosis:** McDonald Criteria. MRI: T2 lesions, Dawson fingers. CSF: oligoclonal bands.

**Treatment:** Acute: IV methylprednisolone. DMTs: interferon, glatiramer, fingolimod, dimethyl fumarate, natalizumab, ocrelizumab (RRMS + PPMS).

#### Migraine
**Definition:** Episodic headache (4-72 hr, unilateral, pulsating, moderate-severe). Affects ~15%.

**Phases:** Prodrome, Aura (visual ~25%), Headache (nausea, photophobia, phonophobia), Postdrome.

**Treatment:** Acute: NSAIDs, triptans, gepants, ditans, antiemetics. Preventive: B-blockers, amitriptyline, topiramate, CGRP mAbs, Botox (chronic).

### 2.3 Respiratory

#### Asthma
**Definition:** Chronic inflammatory airway disease with reversible obstruction and hyperresponsiveness.

**Symptoms:** Episodic wheezing, SOB, chest tightness, cough (nocturnal). Triggers: allergens, viral URI, exercise, cold air, smoke, NSAIDs.

**Diagnosis:** Spirometry: FEV1/FVC <0.70, >=12% & >=200 mL post-bronchodilator improvement. FeNO, methacholine challenge.

**Treatment (GINA):** Step 1: as-needed low-dose ICS-formoterol. Step 2: low-dose ICS + as-needed SABA. Step 3: low-dose ICS-LABA. Step 4: medium-dose ICS-LABA. Step 5: high-dose ICS-LABA + LAMA + biologic (omalizumab, mepolizumab, dupilumab). Exacerbation: SABA + ipratropium, steroids, O2, IV Mg.

#### COPD
**Definition:** Progressive airflow obstruction from smoking. Emphysema + chronic bronchitis.

**Diagnosis:** Spirometry: FEV1/FVC <0.70. GOLD 1-4.

**Treatment:** Smoking cessation (most important). LAMA, LABA, ICS if exacerbations + eosinophilia. Pulmonary rehab. Long-term O2 if PaO2 <=55. Exacerbation: bronchodilators, steroids, antibiotics if purulent, NIV.

#### Pneumonia
**Definition:** Acute lung infection. CAP (S. pneumoniae most common), HAP/VAP (Pseudomonas, MRSA).

**Diagnosis:** CXR, WBC, CRP, procalcitonin, cultures. CURB-65 severity score.

**Treatment (CAP):** Outpatient: amoxicillin or doxycycline or macrolide. Inpatient: ceftriaxone + macrolide or fluoroquinolone. ICU: B-lactam + macrolide or fluoroquinolone. Duration >=5 days.

#### Tuberculosis (TB)
**Definition:** M. tuberculosis. ~10 million cases/year. Airborne.

**Latent TB:** Treated to prevent reactivation (INH 9 mo or rifampin 4 mo or INH+rifapentine 3 mo).

**Active TB:** RIPE (rifampin, INH, pyrazinamide, ethambutol) x2 mo, then INH+rifampin x4 mo. MDR-TB: second-line drugs. DOT recommended.

#### COVID-19
**Definition:** SARS-CoV-2. Pandemic March 2020. Variants: Alpha through Omicron sublineages.

**Pathophysiology:** ACE2 entry -> cytokine storm -> ARDS, hypercoagulability.

**Symptoms:** Fever, cough, anosmia, myalgias, fatigue. Severe: dyspnea, hypoxemia. Long COVID: fatigue, brain fog.

**Diagnosis:** RT-PCR, rapid antigen, CT (ground-glass opacities).

**Treatment:** Mild-high risk: nirmatrelvir/ritonavir (Paxlovid). Severe: remdesivir, dexamethasone, baricitinib, tocilizumab. Anticoagulation. Prevention: vaccines + boosters.

### 2.4 Gastroenterology

#### GERD
**Definition:** Acid reflux causing symptoms or mucosal damage.

**Symptoms:** Heartburn, regurgitation, chronic cough, hoarseness. Alarm: dysphagia, weight loss, bleeding.

**Diagnosis:** Clinical. EGD for alarm or Barrett screening. pH monitoring for refractory.

**Treatment:** Lifestyle (weight loss, elevate HOB, avoid triggers). PPIs first-line. Fundoplication or LINX for refractory.

#### Peptic Ulcer Disease
**Causes:** H. pylori (70% DU, 50% GU) or NSAIDs.

**Symptoms:** DU: pain relieved by food, nocturnal. GU: pain worsened by food.

**Diagnosis:** EGD with biopsy. H. pylori testing (breath test, stool antigen, biopsy).

**Treatment:** H. pylori eradication (triple or quadruple therapy x10-14 days). PPI x4-8 weeks.

#### IBD - Crohn Disease
**Definition:** Transmural inflammation, anywhere GI. Skip lesions, fistulas, strictures.

**Symptoms:** Chronic diarrhea, RLQ pain, weight loss, perianal disease.

**Diagnosis:** Colonoscopy with ileoscopy. MRI enterography.

**Treatment:** Budesonide (mild ileocecal), steroids, anti-TNF (infliximab, adalimumab), vedolizumab, ustekinumab. Immunomodulators for maintenance. Surgery for strictures/fistulas.

#### IBD - Ulcerative Colitis
**Definition:** Continuous mucosal inflammation from rectum proximally.

**Symptoms:** Bloody diarrhea, tenesmus, urgency.

**Diagnosis:** Colonoscopy (continuous from rectum, pseudopolyps).

**Treatment:** Mesalamine (mild-moderate). Steroids, anti-TNF, vedolizumab, tofacitinib for moderate-severe. Total proctocolectomy for refractory.

#### Cirrhosis
**Definition:** Advanced fibrosis with regenerative nodules.

**Causes:** HBV, HCV, alcohol, NASH, autoimmune, PBC, PSC, hemochromatosis, Wilson.

**Complications:** Varices (banding, B-blockers), ascites (diuretics, LVP, TIPS), encephalopathy (lactulose, rifaximin), HCC (screening q6mo ultrasound + AFP), HRS.

**Treatment:** Address cause. Manage complications. Liver transplant for decompensated.

### 2.5 Endocrinology

#### Diabetes Type 1
**Definition:** Autoimmune B-cell destruction -> absolute insulin deficiency. Onset <30 years typically.

**Diagnosis:** Hyperglycemia + positive autoantibodies (GAD65, IA-2) + low C-peptide.

**Treatment:** Insulin: basal (glargine, degludec) + prandial (lispro, aspart, glulisine) or CSII pump. CGM. Target HbA1c <7%. DKA management: IV fluids, IV insulin, K+ repletion, monitor glucose hourly, add D5W when glucose ~250. Transition to SC when eating.

#### Diabetes Type 2
**Definition:** Insulin resistance + progressive B-cell dysfunction. 90% of diabetes. Associated with obesity.

**Diagnosis:** FPG >=126, OGTT 2hr >=200, HbA1c >=6.5%, random >=200 + symptoms.

**Treatment:** Lifestyle + metformin (first-line). Add: SGLT2i (CV/renal benefit), GLP-1 RA (CV benefit, weight loss), DPP-4i, TZD, sulfonylurea, insulin. Target HbA1c <7% (individualized).

**Complications:** Microvascular (retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy) and Macrovascular (CAD, CVD, PAD).

#### Thyroid Disorders
**Hypothyroidism:** Primary (Hashimoto, post-ablative, iodine deficiency). TSH high, free T4 low. Treatment: levothyroxine. Myxedema coma: IV levothyroxine + supportive.

**Hyperthyroidism:** Graves (TSI antibodies, diffuse goiter, orbitopathy). TSH low, T4/T3 high. Treatment: methimazole, RAI, surgery. Thyroid storm: PTU, SSKI, B-blocker, steroids, ICU.

### 2.6 Nephrology

#### Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)
**Definition:** Acute decline in kidney function (hours-days). Stages 1-3.

**Prerenal (60-70%):** Hypovolemia, decreased CO, vasodilation. Reversible. FENa <1%.

**Intrinsic (25-30%):** ATN (ischemia, nephrotoxins), AIN (drugs), GN (vasculitis, lupus, IgA). FENa >2%.

**Postrenal (5-10%):** Obstruction (BPH, stones, tumors).

**Treatment:** Identify cause. Optimize volume. Stop nephrotoxins. Dialysis for refractory hyperkalemia, severe acidosis, volume overload, uremia.

#### Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)
**Definition:** Kidney damage or eGFR <60 for >3 months. Stages 1-5.

**Causes:** Diabetic nephropathy (#1), HTN nephrosclerosis, GN, PCKD.

**Complications:** Anemia (ESA, iron), CKD-MBD (PO4 binders, vitamin D, calcimimetics), metabolic acidosis, CV disease, uremia.

**Treatment:** BP control, ACEi/ARB, SGLT2i, finerenone. Manage complications. Prepare for RRT when eGFR <20-30.

### 2.7 Oncology

#### Breast Cancer
Most common cancer in women worldwide. Subtypes: Luminal A (ER+/PR+/HER2-), Luminal B, HER2-enriched, Triple-negative.

**Diagnosis:** Triple assessment (exam, imaging, biopsy). IHC for ER/PR/HER2/Ki67.

**Treatment:** BCS + radiation or mastectomy. Sentinel node. Chemo (anthracycline + taxane). Endocrine (tamoxifen premenopausal, AI postmenopausal). HER2 therapy (trastuzumab + pertuzumab + T-DM1). Immunotherapy (pembrolizumab for TNBC). CDK4/6 inhibitors for metastatic.

#### Lung Cancer
Leading cancer killer. NSCLC (85%: adenocarcinoma, squamous) and SCLC (15%).

**Diagnosis:** CT, PET, biopsy. Molecular: EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, KRAS, PD-L1, NTRK, RET, MET.

**Treatment:** Surgery, radiation, chemo (platinum doublet), targeted (osimertinib for EGFR, alectinib for ALK), immunotherapy (pembrolizumab, nivolumab).

#### Colorectal Cancer
Third most common. Screening: colonoscopy q10 yr starting age 45.

**Treatment:** Surgery + adjuvant chemo for stage III (FOLFOX, CAPOX). Stage IV: chemo (FOLFOX, FOLFIRI) + targeted (anti-EGFR if RAS wt, bevacizumab) + immunotherapy (pembrolizumab if MSI-H).

#### Prostate Cancer
Most common cancer in men. PSA screening shared decision.

**Treatment:** Active surveillance (low-risk). Prostatectomy or radiation (intermediate). ADT + radiation (high-risk). Metastatic: ADT + abiraterone, enzalutamide, docetaxel.

#### Pancreatic Cancer
Poor prognosis. Smoking, DM, obesity, family history.

**Treatment:** Whipple (resectable). FOLFIRINOX or gemcitabine-based chemo for advanced.

#### Liver Cancer (HCC)
Screen: ultrasound + AFP q6 mo in cirrhotics.

**Treatment:** Resection, ablation, TACE, Y90, systemic (atezolizumab + bevacizumab, sorafenib, lenvatinib). Transplant (Milan criteria).

#### Chemotherapy Principles
**Classes:** Alkylating agents, antimetabolites, anthracyclines, taxanes, platinum, topoisomerase inhibitors, vinca alkaloids.

**Side Effects:** Myelosuppression (nadir 7-14 days), nausea, alopecia, mucositis, fatigue, neuropathy, cardiotoxicity, nephrotoxicity.

**Supportive Care:** Antiemetics (5-HT3 + NK1 antagonists + dexamethasone), G-CSF, growth factors.

#### Immunotherapy
**Checkpoint Inhibitors:** Anti-PD-1 (pembrolizumab, nivolumab), anti-PD-L1, anti-CTLA-4. Side effects: immune-related (colitis, hepatitis, pneumonitis, thyroiditis, myocarditis).

**CAR-T:** Anti-CD19 for ALL/DLBCL, anti-BCMA for myeloma. Side effects: cytokine release syndrome, ICANS.

### 2.8 Infectious Disease

#### HIV/AIDS
**Definition:** HIV infects CD4+ T cells -> immunodeficiency. 38 million living with HIV.

**Diagnosis:** 4th gen Ag/Ab test. Confirm with viral load.

**Treatment:** ART: NRTI backbone (TAF/FTC or ABC/3TC) + INSTI (dolutegravir, bictegravir). U=U.

**Prophylaxis:** PrEP (TAF/FTC for high-risk). PEP (3-drug ART x28 days). OI prophylaxis (TMP-SMX for PCP if CD4<200).

#### Hepatitis B
**Definition:** HBV - acute or chronic. Chronic in 5% adults, 90% neonates.

**Diagnosis:** HBsAg (active), HBeAg (replication), HBV DNA.

**Treatment:** Tenofovir, entecavir, peg-IFN. Monitor for HCC. Prevention: vaccine.

#### Hepatitis C
**Definition:** HCV - chronic in 70-85%. DAA cure >95% (sofosbuvir/velpatasvir, glecaprevir/pibrentasvir x8-12 wk).

#### Malaria
**Definition:** Plasmodium (P. falciparum most severe). Anopheles mosquitoes. ~240M cases, ~600K deaths/year.

**Symptoms:** Cyclic fever, chills, headache, myalgias, anemia. Severe: cerebral malaria, ARDS, AKI, DIC.

**Treatment:** ACT (artemether-lumefantrine) for uncomplicated P. falciparum. IV artesunate for severe. Chloroquine + primaquine for P. vivax (test G6PD).

#### Sepsis
**Definition:** Life-threatening organ dysfunction from infection. Septic shock: vasopressors needed.

**Diagnosis:** qSOFA (AMS, RR>=22, SBP<=100). Lactate, blood cultures, procalcitonin.

**Treatment:** 1 hr bundle: blood cultures, broad-spectrum antibiotics, 30 mL/kg crystalloid, vasopressors (norepinephrine), source control.

#### Antibiotic Resistance
**Major Threats:** MRSA, VRE, ESBL, CRE, MDR Pseudomonas, MDR Acinetobacter.

**Prevention:** Antibiotic stewardship, infection control, hand hygiene, vaccination.

### 2.9 Psychiatry

#### Major Depressive Disorder
**Definition:** Depressed mood or anhedonia >=2 wk + >=5 symptoms: sleep change, appetite/weight change, fatigue, psychomotor change, worthlessness, concentration difficulty, suicidal thoughts.

**Treatment:** SSRIs (fluoxetine, sertraline, escitalopram) first-line. SNRIs (venlafaxine, duloxetine). Bupropion, mirtazapine. TCAs, MAOIs. ECT for severe/refractory. TMS, ketamine/esketamine.

#### Anxiety Disorders
**GAD:** Excessive worry >=6 mo + restlessness, fatigue, concentration difficulty, irritability, muscle tension, sleep disturbance.

**Panic Disorder:** Recurrent unexpected panic attacks (palpitations, sweating, trembling, SOB, chest pain, nausea, dizziness, fear of dying/losing control) + fear of future attacks.

**Treatment:** SSRIs/SNRIs first-line. CBT. Benzodiazepines short-term only. Buspirone, pregabalin.

#### Bipolar Disorder
**Definition:** Manic episodes (elevated mood, grandiosity, decreased sleep need, pressured speech, racing thoughts, increased activity, poor judgment) +/or depressive episodes.

**Treatment:** Lithium (gold standard), valproate, lamotrigine. Atypical antipsychotics (quetiapine, olanzapine, aripiprazole). Avoid antidepressant monotherapy.

#### Schizophrenia
**Definition:** Chronic psychosis. Positive (delusions, hallucinations, disorganization), negative (avolition, social withdrawal, anhedonia), cognitive symptoms.

**Treatment:** First-gen antipsychotics (haloperidol - high EPS). Second-gen (olanzapine, risperidone, quetiapine, aripiprazole, clozapine for refractory - agranulocytosis risk). Psychosocial interventions.

#### OCD
**Definition:** Obsessions (intrusive thoughts) + compulsions (repetitive behaviors). Treatment: CBT-ERP + SSRIs (high dose) or clomipramine.

#### PTSD
**Definition:** After trauma: re-experiencing, avoidance, negative cognitions, hyperarousal >=1 mo. Treatment: SSRIs, prazosin for nightmares, CBT, EMDR.

#### ADHD
**Definition:** Inattention, hyperactivity, impulsivity. Onset <12 yr, >=2 settings. Treatment: stimulants (methylphenidate, amphetamine) first-line. Atomoxetine, guanfacine.

#### Substance Use Disorders
**Alcohol:** Detox (benzodiazepines), MAT (naltrexone, acamprosate, disulfiram).

**Opioids:** MAT (methadone, buprenorphine, naltrexone). Naloxone for overdose reversal.

### 2.10 Dermatology

#### Acne Vulgaris
**Treatment:** Mild: topical retinoids + benzoyl peroxide +/- topical antibiotics. Moderate: oral antibiotics (doxycycline, minocycline). Severe: isotretinoin (monitor lipids, LFTs, pregnancy prevention).

#### Eczema (Atopic Dermatitis)
**Treatment:** Emollients, topical corticosteroids, topical calcineurin inhibitors. Severe: phototherapy, cyclosporine, methotrexate, dupilumab.

#### Psoriasis
**Treatment:** Topical corticosteroids + vitamin D analogs (calcipotriene). Phototherapy (UVB). Systemic: methotrexate, cyclosporine, acitretin. Biologics: TNF-inhibitors, IL-17 inhibitors (secukinumab), IL-23 inhibitors (ustekinumab, guselkumab).

#### Skin Cancer
**BCC:** Pearly papule with telangiectasias. Rarely metastasizes. Excision or Mohs.

**SCC:** Actinic keratosis precursor. Can metastasize. Excision, Mohs, radiation.

**Melanoma:** ABCDE. Breslow depth predicts prognosis. Wide excision + SLNB. Advanced: immunotherapy (anti-PD-1, anti-CTLA-4), BRAF/MEK inhibitors if BRAF V600 mutant.

### 2.11 Orthopedics

#### Fractures
**Types:** Closed, open (IV antibiotics + urgent I&D), comminuted, greenstick (children), stress, pathologic.

**Diagnosis:** X-ray (2 views, joint above/below). CT for complex. Neurovascular exam essential.

**Treatment:** Reduction (closed or open), immobilization (cast, splint, external fixator), internal fixation (plates, screws, IM nail, K-wires).

#### Osteoarthritis (OA)
**Definition:** Degenerative joint disease. Cartilage loss, bone spurs. Weight-bearing joints, hands, spine.

**Symptoms:** Joint pain with use, morning stiffness <30 min, crepitus, decreased ROM.

**Treatment:** Weight loss, exercise, PT, braces, analgesics (acetaminophen, NSAIDs). Joint injections (steroids, hyaluronic acid). Joint replacement for severe.

#### Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA)
**Definition:** Chronic autoimmune inflammatory arthritis. Symmetric small joints (MCP, PIP, MTP), wrists, elbows, knees. Extra-articular: nodules, lung fibrosis, vasculitis.

**Diagnosis:** Clinical + RF, anti-CCP, CRP/ESR. X-ray: erosions, joint space narrowing.

**Treatment:** DMARDs: methotrexate (anchor drug), sulfasalazine, leflunomide, hydroxychloroquine. Biologics: TNF-inhibitors (etanercept, adalimumab, infliximab), IL-6 inhibitors (tocilizumab), JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib, baricitinib), rituximab, abatacept.

### 2.12 Pediatrics

#### Vaccinations (US CDC Schedule)
**Birth:** HepB. 2 mo: HepB, RV, DTaP, Hib, PCV13, IPV. 4 mo: RV, DTaP, Hib, PCV13, IPV. 6 mo: HepB, RV, DTaP, Hib, PCV13, IPV, flu (annually). 12-15 mo: MMR, varicella, HepA, PCV13, Hib. 18 mo: DTaP, HepA. 4-6 yr: DTaP, IPV, MMR, varicella. 11-12 yr: HPV (2 doses), MenACWY, Tdap, flu. 16 yr: MenACWY booster. 16-18 yr: MenB (shared decision).

#### Common Childhood Illnesses
**Otitis Media:** Fever, ear pain, bulging TM. Amoxicillin 10 days or observation if >6 mo mild.

**Bronchiolitis (RSV):** Wheezing, respiratory distress <12 mo. Supportive care (O2, hydration, suctioning).

**Croup:** Barking cough, stridor (parainfluenza). Dexamethasone 0.6 mg/kg single dose. Nebulized epinephrine for severe stridor.

**Kawasaki Disease:** Fever >=5 days + conjunctivitis, oral changes, rash, extremity changes, cervical LAD. Echo for coronary aneurysms. IVIG + high-dose aspirin.

### 2.13 OB/GYN

#### Menstrual Disorders
**Amenorrhea:** Primary (no menses by 15) or Secondary (3 mo no menses). Causes: pregnancy, PCOS, hypothalamic, hyperprolactinemia, ovarian failure.

**PCOS:** Oligo/anovulation + hyperandrogenism + polycystic ovaries. Associated: insulin resistance, obesity. Treatment: OCPs, metformin, letrozole for fertility.

**Dysmenorrhea:** Primary (prostaglandin). NSAIDs, OCPs.

#### Endometriosis
**Definition:** Endometrial tissue outside uterus. Pain (cyclical, chronic), dyspareunia, infertility.

**Diagnosis:** Clinical + ultrasound. Definitive: laparoscopy.

**Treatment:** NSAIDs, OCPs, progestins, GnRH agonists, surgical excision, IVF for fertility.

#### Cervical Cancer
**Definition:** HPV-related (16, 18). Screening: Pap + HPV co-testing age 21-65. Colposcopy + biopsy for abnormal.

**Treatment:** LEEP for CIN. Hysterectomy or chemoradiation for invasive.

**Prevention:** HPV vaccine (9-valent, age 9-26, catch-up to 45).

#### Pregnancy & Childbirth
**Prenatal:** Folic acid, dating ultrasound, NIPT, NT (11-14 wk), anatomy scan (18-22 wk), GDM screen (24-28 wk).

**Preeclampsia:** HTN + proteinuria after 20 wk. Treatment: delivery, MgSO4 seizure prophylaxis, antihypertensives (labetalol, hydralazine, nifedipine).

**Labor:** Stage 1 (cervical dilation), Stage 2 (delivery), Stage 3 (placenta).

#### Infertility
**Definition:** Failure to conceive after 12 months (6 months if >35). Causes: male (low sperm count, motility), female (ovulatory, tubal, uterine, endometriosis), unexplained.

**Evaluation:** Semen analysis, ovulation assessment, hysterosalpingogram, ovarian reserve (AMH, FSH). Treatment: ovulation induction (clomiphene, letrozole, gonadotropins), IUI, IVF, ICSI.

### 2.14 Ophthalmology

#### Cataracts
**Definition:** Lens opacification. Age-related most common.

**Symptoms:** Painless progressive vision loss, glare, halos.

**Treatment:** Phacoemulsification + IOL when vision impairment affects daily activities.

#### Glaucoma
**Definition:** Optic neuropathy with visual field loss, elevated IOP.

**POAG:** Asymptomatic until late. Prostaglandin analogs first-line. Laser trabeculoplasty, trabeculectomy.

**Acute Angle-Closure:** Painful red eye, fixed mid-dilated pupil, corneal edema, IOP >40. Emergency. Treatment: topical/systemic IOP lowering + laser iridotomy.

#### Age-Related Macular Degeneration (AMD)
**Dry (90%):** Drusen, gradual vision loss. AREDS2 vitamins.

**Wet (10%):** Neovascular, severe vision loss. Anti-VEGF injections (ranibizumab, aflibercept, bevacizumab).

#### Diabetic Retinopathy
**NPDR -> PDR** (neovascularization, vitreous hemorrhage). Macular edema.

**Treatment:** Tight glycemic/BP/lipid control. PRP for PDR. Anti-VEGF for macular edema.

### 2.15 ENT

#### Otitis Media
**Acute:** Fever, ear pain, bulging TM. Amoxicillin 10 days.

**OME:** Fluid without infection. Observation. Tympanostomy tubes if >3 mo with hearing loss.

#### Sinusitis
**Viral:** Supportive. **Bacterial:** Symptoms >10 days or double worsening. Amoxicillin-clavulanate 5-7 days.

#### Hearing Loss
**Conductive (outer/middle ear):** Wax, otitis media, otosclerosis, TM perforation.

**Sensorineural (cochlea/nerve):** Presbycusis, noise, Meniere, ototoxic, acoustic neuroma.

**Treatment:** Conductive: treat cause. SNHL: hearing aids, cochlear implants.

#### Vertigo
**Peripheral:** BPPV (Epley maneuver), vestibular neuritis (vestibular suppressants + steroids), Meniere (low Na diet, diuretics).

**Central:** TIA/stroke (posterior circulation), migraine, MS. Examine for neurologic signs.

### 2.16 Rheumatology

#### Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE)
**Definition:** Chronic autoimmune, multi-organ. Women childbearing age, African/Hispanic/Asian.

**Diagnosis:** Malar rash, discoid rash, photosensitivity, oral ulcers, arthritis, serositis, nephritis, neurologic, hematologic + ANA (>95%), anti-dsDNA, anti-Smith, anti-Ro/La, anti-phospholipid.

**Treatment:** Hydroxychloroquine (all patients). Steroids + immunosuppressants (MMF, cyclophosphamide, azathioprine) for organ-threatening. Belimumab. Avoid UV.

#### Gout
**Definition:** Monosodium urate crystals from hyperuricemia.

**Acute:** Severe pain/redness/swelling (podagra 50-60%). NSAIDs, colchicine, steroids.

**Chronic:** Allopurinol (start after acute resolves, low and slow), febuxostat, probenecid.

#### Ankylosing Spondylitis
**Definition:** Axial spondyloarthritis. HLA-B27 associated. Inflammatory back pain, sacroiliitis.

**Treatment:** NSAIDs first-line. TNF-inhibitors (infliximab, adalimumab). IL-17 inhibitors (secukinumab). PT.

### 2.17 Hematology

#### Anemia
**Definition:** Reduced RBC mass or hemoglobin. Normocytic (anemia of chronic disease, hemolytic, acute blood loss), Microcytic (iron deficiency, thalassemia, anemia of chronic disease), Macrocytic (B12 deficiency, folate deficiency, alcohol, MDS, hypothyroidism).

**Diagnosis:** CBC, reticulocyte count, peripheral smear, iron studies (Fe, TIBC, ferritin), B12, folate, hemolysis labs (LDH, haptoglobin, indirect bilirubin).

**Treatment:** Iron deficiency: oral or IV iron. B12 deficiency: IM B12. Folate deficiency: oral folate. Anemia of chronic disease: treat underlying disease, consider ESA. Hemolytic: steroids, splenectomy, immunosuppression.

#### Thrombocytopenia
**Causes:** ITP (autoimmune, isolated low platelets), TTP/HUS, DIC, drug-induced, hypersplenism, marrow suppression.

**ITP:** Petechiae, bruising, bleeding. Diagnosis of exclusion. Treatment: steroids, IVIG, TPO-agonists (eltrombopag, romiplostim), rituximab, splenectomy.

#### Coagulation Disorders
**Hemophilia A:** Factor VIII deficiency, X-linked. Hemarthroses, bleeding. Treatment: factor VIII replacement.

**Hemophilia B:** Factor IX deficiency. Factor IX replacement.

**von Willebrand Disease:** Most common inherited. Mucocutaneous bleeding, menorrhagia. Treatment: DDAVP, VWF-containing concentrates, TXA.

**DIC:** Widespread coagulation activation from sepsis, trauma, malignancy, obstetrical complications. Microvascular thrombosis + consumption coagulopathy -> bleeding. Treat underlying cause. Support with platelets, FFP, cryoprecipitate.

#### Venous Thromboembolism (VTE)
**Definition:** DVT + PE. Virchow triad: stasis, hypercoagulability, endothelial injury.

**Risk Factors:** Surgery, immobilization, cancer, pregnancy, OCPs/HRT, inherited (Factor V Leiden, prothrombin mutation, protein C/S deficiency, antithrombin deficiency), antiphospholipid syndrome, age, obesity.

**Diagnosis:** DVT: compression ultrasound. PE: CTPA (pulmonary angiogram CT), V/Q scan. D-dimer (high sensitivity, low specificity).

**Treatment:** Anticoagulation: LMWH or fondaparinux initially, transition to warfarin (INR 2-3) or DOAC (rivaroxaban, apixaban, edoxaban, dabigatran). Duration: 3-6 months for provoked, indefinite for unprovoked or recurrent. Thrombolysis for massive PE with hemodynamic instability.

#### Multiple Myeloma
**Definition:** Plasma cell malignancy. Monoclonal gammopathy. CRAB: hyperCalcemia, Renal failure, Anemia, Bone lesions (lytic).

**Diagnosis:** Serum/urine protein electrophoresis + immunofixation, serum free light chains, bone marrow biopsy (>10% plasma cells), skeletal survey (lytic lesions).

**Treatment:** Induction (VRd: bortezomib, lenalidomide, dexamethasone) followed by autologous stem cell transplant. Maintenance (lenalidomide). Newer: daratumumab (anti-CD38), CAR-T (idecabtagene vicleucel, ciltacabtagene autoleucel), bispecific antibodies.

#### Lymphoma
**Hodgkin Lymphoma:** Reed-Sternberg cells. Painless lymphadenopathy (cervical/mediastinal), B symptoms (fever, night sweats, weight loss). Stages I-IV. Treatment: ABVD chemo + radiation for early stage. Brentuximab vedotin, checkpoint inhibitors for relapsed.

**Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma:** Heterogeneous. DLBCL (most common, aggressive): R-CHOP. Follicular (indolent): watchful waiting or rituximab-based. MALT, Mantle cell, Burkitt, T-cell lymphomas. CAR-T for relapsed DLBCL.

#### Leukemia
**ALL:** Children peak (B-cell precursor). Treatment: multi-agent chemo (vincristine, steroids, anthracycline, asparaginase, intrathecal methotrexate). CNS prophylaxis. Tyrosine kinase inhibitors (imatinib, dasatinib) for Ph+ ALL. CAR-T for relapsed.

**AML:** Older adults. Subtypes by morphology (FAB) and genetics. Treatment: induction (7+3: cytarabine x7 days + anthracycline x3 days) followed by consolidation. Targeted: FLT3 inhibitors (midostaurin, quizartinib), IDH inhibitors (ivosidenib, enasidenib), gemtuzumab ozogamicin (anti-CD33).

**CLL:** Older adults. Asymptomatic: watchful waiting. Treatment: BTK inhibitors (ibrutinib, acalabrutinib, zanubrutinib), BCL2 inhibitors (venetoclax) + anti-CD20 (obinutuzumab, rituximab). Chemo (FCR) for younger/fit.

**CML:** Philadelphia chromosome (BCR-ABL). Three phases: chronic, accelerated, blast. Treatment: TKIs (imatinib, dasatinib, nilotinib, bosutinib, ponatinib). Monitor BCR-ABL levels. Allo-SCT for resistant.

## 3. Diagnostic Methods

### 3.1 Physical Examination

**General:** Vital signs (BP, HR, RR, Temp, SpO2), general appearance, distress, nutrition, hydration.

**HEENT:** Fundoscopy (optic disc, vessels, retina, papilledema), conjunctiva (pale, injected), sclera (icterus), pupil (size, symmetry, light reflex), extraocular movements, cranial nerves II-XII, thyroid palpation, oral mucosa, pharynx, tonsils.

**Cardiovascular:** Inspection (visible pulsations, scars, chest deformity), Palpation (PMI location/quality, thrills, heaves), Auscultation (S1, S2, S3/S4 gallops, murmurs [timing, location, radiation, intensity/grade 1-6, quality, pitch], rubs). Carotid upstroke, JVP assessment, peripheral pulses, edema, capillary refill.

**Respiratory:** Inspection (respiratory rate, pattern, accessory muscle use, chest wall symmetry, scars), Palpation (tactile fremitus, tenderness, expansion), Percussion (resonant, dull [consolidation/effusion], hyperresonant [COPD/pneumothorax]), Auscultation (breath sounds: vesicular/bronchial/bronchovesicular/adventitious: crackles/rales, wheezes, rhonchi, pleural rub). Bronchophony, egophony (E to A change), whispered pectoriloquy.

**Abdominal:** Inspection (distention, scars, hernias, caput medusae, visible peristalsis), Auscultation (bowel sounds, bruits), Percussion (tympany, shifting dullness for ascites, liver span), Palpation (light then deep, organomegaly [liver, spleen, kidneys], masses, tenderness, guarding, rebound, Murphy sign [cholecystitis], McBurney point [appendicitis], Roysing sign, psoas/obturator signs). Rectal exam (masses, prostate, stool occult blood).

**Neurologic:** Mental status (MMSE, MoCA), Cranial nerves I-XII, Motor (tone, bulk, strength 0-5 scale, pronator drift), Reflexes (DTRs: 0-4+, Babinski, Hoffman), Coordination (finger-to-nose, heel-to-shin, rapid alternating movements, Romberg), Sensory (light touch, pinprick, temperature, vibration, proprioception, cortical sensory), Gait (tandem, heel, toe, Romberg).

**Musculoskeletal:** Inspection (deformity, swelling, erythema, atrophy), Palpation (tenderness, warmth, crepitus, effusion), Range of motion (active and passive), Special tests (Lachman/ACL, McMurray/meniscus, impingement/shoulder, FABERE/hip).

### 3.2 Laboratory Tests

**Complete Blood Count (CBC):** WBC (4.5-11.0 x10^9/L), RBC, Hb (M 13.5-17.5, F 12.0-16.0 g/dL), Hct (M 39-49%, F 35-45%), MCV (80-100 fL), MCH, MCHC, RDW, platelets (150-400 x10^9/L), differential (neutrophils 40-70%, lymphocytes 20-40%, monocytes 2-8%, eosinophils 1-4%, basophils 0.5-1%).

**Basic Metabolic Panel (BMP):** Na (135-145), K (3.5-5.0), Cl (98-106), HCO3 (22-28), BUN (7-20), Cr (0.6-1.2), Glucose (70-110), Ca (8.5-10.5).

**Comprehensive Metabolic Panel (CMP):** BMP + total protein, albumin, ALP, AST, ALT, bilirubin (total/direct).

**Coagulation:** PT (11-13.5 sec), INR (0.8-1.2), aPTT (25-35 sec), fibrinogen (200-400 mg/dL).

**Cardiac Enzymes:** Troponin I (normal <0.04 ng/mL, highly sensitive cTn <14 ng/L), CK-MB, BNP (<100 pg/mL), NT-proBNP (<125 pg/mL).

**Tumor Markers:** AFP (HCC, germ cell), CA-125 (ovarian), CA 19-9 (pancreatic), CEA (colorectal), PSA (prostate), HCG (germ cell, choriocarcinoma), LDH (germ cell, lymphoma, melanoma), calcitonin (medullary thyroid).

**Blood Cultures:** 2 sets from separate sites (each: aerobic + anaerobic bottle, 10-20 mL blood). Sensitivity 50-70% in sepsis. Contamination common (false positives).

**Arterial Blood Gas (ABG):** pH (7.35-7.45), PaCO2 (35-45), PaO2 (80-100), HCO3 (22-28), Base excess (-2 to +2), O2 saturation (>95%). Respiratory vs metabolic acidosis/alkalosis. Anion gap = Na - (Cl + HCO3) = 8-12. Osmolar gap.

**Urinalysis:** Dipstick (pH, specific gravity, glucose, ketones, blood, protein, nitrites, leukocyte esterase, bilirubin, urobilinogen). Microscopy (RBCs, WBCs, casts [RBC, WBC, granular, hyaline], crystals, bacteria, yeast, epithelial cells).

### 3.3 Imaging

**X-Ray:** Chest (PA + lateral: heart size, lung fields, pleural spaces, bones, mediastinum, hila). Abdominal (free air under diaphragm, obstruction air-fluid levels, calcifications). Extremity (fracture, dislocation, arthritis, foreign body). Spine (compression fracture, alignment, degenerative changes). Radiation dose low (~0.02-0.1 mSv).

**CT (Computed Tomography):** Cross-sectional imaging with ionizing radiation. Non-contrast (stones, bleeding, calcification). IV contrast (enhances vascular structures, parenchymal lesions, abscesses). Oral/rectal contrast (bowel). Applications: head (trauma, stroke, bleed), chest (PE, lung nodule, mass, pneumonia), abdomen/pelvis (appendicitis, diverticulitis, abscess, tumor, trauma, obstruction), spine (fracture, stenosis). Dose ~2-20 mSv.

**MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging):** No ionizing radiation. Superior soft tissue contrast. T1 (anatomy), T2 (pathology: water bright), FLAIR (periventricular lesions), DWI (acute stroke), contrast (gadolinium: enhancement, but NSF risk with renal failure). Applications: brain/spine (tumor, MS, stroke, infection), MSK (ligament/tendon, meniscus, cartilage, bone marrow edema, avascular necrosis), pelvis (prostate, uterine, ovarian), abdomen (liver lesion characterization, MRCP for bile ducts). Contraindications: incompatible implants, claustrophobia, gadolinium in CKD.

**Ultrasound:** No radiation, real-time, portable. Uses sound waves (high frequency 2-18 MHz). Applications: abdominal (gallstones, biliary dilatation, liver masses, ascites, renal stones, hydronephrosis, AAA screening), cardiac (echocardiogram: LVEF, wall motion, valves, pericardium, congenital), pelvic (uterus, ovaries, pregnancy: dating, anatomy, growth), vascular (carotid stenosis, DVT, arterial insufficiency, vein mapping), MSK (joint effusion, tendon tear, guided injection), FAST (focused assessment with sonography in trauma: free fluid). Limitations: operator-dependent, limited by gas/bone/obesity, poor penetration.

**PET (Positron Emission Tomography):** Metabolic imaging with FDG (fluorodeoxyglucose - glucose analog, taken up by metabolically active cells). Combined with CT (PET/CT) for anatomic localization. Applications: cancer staging/restaging (lung, lymphoma, melanoma, colorectal, head/neck, esophageal), myocardial viability, infection/inflammation, dementia (amyloid PET, FDG-PET). Limitations: false positive in infection/inflammation, false negative in low-grade/low-GLU tumors, high cost, radiation.

**Nuclear Medicine:** Radioactive tracers for functional imaging. Bone scan (Tc-99m MDP: metastases, fracture, osteomyelitis, Paget). V/Q scan (ventilation/perfusion for PE when CTPA contraindicated). Thyroid scan (Tc-99m pertechnetate or I-123: nodules, Graves, thyroiditis). HIDA scan (Tc-99m DISIDA: acute cholecystitis, bile leak). DMSA scan (renal cortical scarring). MUGA (cardiac function). I-131 MIBG (neuroblastoma, pheochromocytoma).

### 3.4 ECG Interpretation

**Standard 12-Lead ECG:** Limb leads (I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF) and precordial leads (V1-V6). Paper speed 25 mm/s (1 small box = 0.04 sec, 1 large box = 0.20 sec). Voltage: 10 mm = 1 mV.

**Systematic Interpretation:**
1. Rate (normal 60-100, brady <60, tachy >100)
2. Rhythm (regular, irregularly irregular, regularly irregular)
3. Axis (normal -30 to +90, left deviation -30 to -90, right deviation +90 to +180)
4. P wave (sinus: upright in II, inverted in aVR; atrial enlargement: P mitrale [M-shaped], P pulmonale [peaked])
5. PR interval (0.12-0.20 sec, prolonged in 1st degree AV block)
6. QRS complex (normal <0.12 sec, widened in bundle branch block, ventricular rhythms)
7. Q wave (pathologic: >1 box wide, >1/3 QRS height, in 2+ contiguous leads -> old MI)
8. ST segment (elevation: STEMI, pericarditis, early repolarization; depression: ischemia, strain, digoxin)
9. T wave (inverted: ischemia, strain, digoxin, PE; peaked: hyperkalemia; flattened: hypokalemia)
10. QT interval (normal <440 ms men, <460 ms women, prolonged: risk of torsades de pointes)
11. U wave (hypokalemia)

**Common Patterns:**
- **LVH:** S V1 + R V5/V6 >35 mm, strain pattern (ST depression + T inversion in lateral leads)
- **RBBB:** rsR' in V1, wide S in V6
- **LBBB:** deep S in V1, wide R in V6, QS in V1
- **Atrial Fibrillation:** absent P waves, irregularly irregular
- **Atrial Flutter:** sawtooth flutter waves (250-350 bpm)
- **VT:** wide QRS, AV dissociation, capture/fusion beats
- **Pericarditis:** diffuse ST elevation (concave upward), PR depression
- **Hyperkalemia:** peaked T waves, widened QRS, sine wave -> arrest
- **Hypokalemia:** flattened T waves, U waves, prolonged QT

### 3.5 Endoscopic Procedures

**EGD (Upper Endoscopy):** Visualizes esophagus, stomach, duodenum. Sedation (moderate: midazolam + fentanyl/propofol). Indications: dysphagia, GERD refractory, GI bleeding (variceal vs non-variceal), suspicious lesions, foreign body removal, biopsy (Barretts, H. pylori, celiac, malignancy). Therapeutic: polypectomy, mucosal resection, cautery, banding, dilation, PEG tube placement.

**Colonoscopy:** Visualizes rectum to cecum (terminal ileum ideally). Bowel prep (polyethylene glycol, osmotic laxatives). Sedation. Indications: screening (starting age 45, q10 yr), diagnostic (bleeding, chronic diarrhea, IBD surveillance, anemia, change in bowel habits). Therapeutic: polypectomy (cold snare/hot snare, EMR), biopsy, cautery, dilation.

**Bronchoscopy:** Flexible scope into tracheobronchial tree. Indications: lung mass/nodule biopsy (EBUS-TBNA for mediastinal/hilar LAD), hemoptysis evaluation, persistent pneumonia, foreign body removal, BAL (infection, hemorrhage, eosinophilia). Complications: bleeding, pneumothorax, laryngospasm, hypoxia.

**ERCP (Endoscopic Retrograde Cholangiopancreatography):** Cannulates bile duct and/or pancreatic duct. Indications: choledocholithiasis (stones), biliary stricture, leak, stent placement, sphincterotomy. Complications: pancreatitis (5-10%, most common), perforation, bleeding, cholangitis.

**Endoscopic Ultrasound (EUS):** Ultrasound probe on endoscope for detailed imaging of GI wall and adjacent structures. Pancreatic masses/cysts, bile duct stones, mediastinal LN, subepithelial lesions, tumor staging (TNM). Fine needle aspiration for cytology.

### 3.6 Other Diagnostic Procedures

**Lumbar Puncture:** CSF collection from L3-L5 (conus medullaris at L1-L2 adults). Indications: meningitis/encephalitis, SAH (CT negative), demyelinating disease (OCBs), carcinomatous meningitis, idiopathic intracranial hypertension (pressure measurement). Contraindications: increased ICP (mass lesion, obstruction, risk of herniation), coagulopathy, infection at site. CSF analysis: cell count/diff, protein, glucose (compare to serum), Gram stain, culture, PCR (viral/bacterial/TB/fungal), cytology.

**Paracentesis:** Ascitic fluid sampling from abdominal cavity. Indications: new ascites evaluation, suspected SBP. Analysis: cell count (PMN >=250 = SBP), albumin (SAAG >=1.1 = portal HTN), culture, cytology, Gram stain, glucose, LDH, amylase.

**Thoracentesis:** Pleural fluid sampling. Indications: pleural effusion evaluation. Analysis: Light criteria (exudate if: protein pleural/serum >0.5, LDH pleural/serum >0.6, LDH pleural >2/3 upper limit serum). Cell count, cytology, culture, Gram stain, pH (<7.2 malignant/infectious, <7.0 empyema), glucose, amylase, triglycerides.

**Bone Marrow Biopsy:** Iliac crest (posterior superior). Indications: unexplained cytopenias, leukemia/lymphoma diagnosis/staging, myeloma, MDS, aplastic anemia, storage diseases, metastases. Aspirate: morphology, flow cytometry, cytogenetics, molecular studies. Core biopsy: cellularity, fibrosis, architecture, metastases.

**Biopsy Techniques:** Core needle (large tissue samples), Fine needle aspiration (cytology), Incisional (part of lesion), Excisional (entire lesion), Punch biopsy (skin), Shave biopsy (skin). Image guidance (US, CT, MRI) for deep lesions.

## 4. Treatment Modalities

### 4.1 Pharmacology

**Drug Classes (Major):**

**Antibiotics:**
- **Penicillins:** Amoxicillin, ampicillin, amoxicillin-clavulanate, piperacillin-tazobactam. B-lactamase inhibitors extend spectrum. Side effects: hypersensitivity (1-10%, anaphylaxis 0.01-0.05%), GI upset, C. diff.
- **Cephalosporins:** 1st gen (cefazolin, cephalexin - skin/soft tissue/prophylaxis), 2nd gen (cefoxitin, cefuroxime - intra-abdominal/community pneumonia), 3rd gen (ceftriaxone [CNS penetration, gonorrhea], cefotaxime, ceftazidime [Pseudomonas], cefixime), 4th gen (cefepime - Gram-neg + some Gram-pos), 5th gen (ceftaroline - MRSA).
- **Carbapenems:** Imipenem, meropenem, ertapenem, doripenem. Broadest spectrum (Gram-pos, Gram-neg, anaerobes). Reserved for MDR/ESBL infections. Meropenem has least seizure risk.
- **Macrolides:** Azithromycin, clarithromycin, erythromycin. Atypicals coverage (Mycoplasma, Chlamydia, Legionella). QT prolongation risk.
- **Fluoroquinolones:** Ciprofloxacin (Gram-neg, Pseudomonas), levofloxacin, moxifloxacin (respiratory + atypicals). Side effects: tendinitis/tendon rupture (FDA black box), QT prolongation, C. diff, neuropathy, CNS effects.
- **Aminoglycosides:** Gentamicin, tobramycin, amikacin. Synergistic with B-lactams for Gram-negatives. Nephrotoxic + ototoxic. Therapeutic drug monitoring (peak/trough levels).
- **Tetracyclines:** Doxycycline, minocycline. Atypicals, acne, malaria prophylaxis, MRSA skin. Photosensitivity, tooth discoloration (children/pregnancy).
- **Glycopeptides:** Vancomycin. MRSA, C. diff (oral). Nephrotoxic + ototoxic. Monitor trough levels.
- **Oxazolidinones:** Linezolid. MRSA, VRE. Myelosuppression (monitor CBC), serotonin syndrome (MAOI interaction).
- **Lipopeptides:** Daptomycin. MRSA, VRE. Inactivated by surfactant (not for pneumonia). Myopathy (monitor CK).
- **Metronidazole:** Anaerobes, C. diff, protozoa. Metallic taste, disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol.
- **Clindamycin:** Anaerobes, Gram-pos, bone infections. High C. diff risk.
- **TMP-SMX:** PCP prophylaxis/treatment, UTI, MRSA. Hyperkalemia, sulfa allergy, marrow suppression.
- **Nitrofurantoin:** Uncomplicated UTI only (concentrated in urine). Pulmonary fibrosis (chronic use).

**Antihypertensives:**
- **ACEi (lisinopril, ramipril, enalapril):** Block angiotensin II formation. Cough (10-20%), angioedema (rare), hyperkalemia, teratogenic.
- **ARB (losartan, valsartan, olmesartan):** Block AT1 receptor. No cough, otherwise similar.
- **CCB:** Dihydropyridine (amlodipine, nifedipine - vasodilation, edema). Non-dihydropyridine (verapamil, diltiazem - rate control, constipation).
- **Thiazides:** Hydrochlorothiazide (HCTZ), chlorthalidone. Hypokalemia, hyponatremia, hyperuricemia, hyperglycemia.
- **B-blockers:** Metoprolol, atenolol, carvedilol (non-selective + alpha-blocking), bisoprolol, propranolol. Bradycardia, heart block, fatigue, bronchospasm (non-selective), masking hypoglycemia.
- **MRA:** Spironolactone, eplerenone. Hyperkalemia, gynecomastia (spironolactone).
- **Alpha-blockers:** Doxazosin, tamsulosin (selective alpha-1A for BPH). Orthostatic hypotension, especially first dose.
- **Direct vasodilators:** Hydralazine (lupus-like syndrome), minoxidil (hypertrichosis).

**Antidiabetic Agents:**
- **Metformin:** Decreases hepatic gluconeogenesis, increases insulin sensitivity. Lactic acidosis risk (rare, avoid eGFR <30), GI side effects.
- **SGLT2i (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin):** Block renal glucose reabsorption. CV/renal benefit, GU infections, DKA (euglycemic), amputations (canagliflozin caution).
- **GLP-1 RA (semaglutide, liraglutide, dulaglutide):** Increase GLP-1, slow gastric emptying, promote weight loss, CV benefit. Nausea, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease.
- **DPP-4i (sitagliptin, linagliptin):** Increase endogenous GLP-1. Minimal side effects, modest efficacy.
- **TZD (pioglitazone):** Insulin sensitizer. Weight gain, edema, fractures, bladder cancer concern.
- **Sulfonylureas (glipizide, glimepiride):** Increase insulin secretion. Weight gain, hypoglycemia.
- **Insulin:** Basal (glargine, detemir, degludec, NPH), Prandial (lispro, aspart, glulisine, regular), Premixed. Side effects: hypoglycemia, weight gain.

**Antidepressants:**
- **SSRIs (fluoxetine, sertraline, escitalopram, paroxetine, citalopram):** First-line. Side effects: GI upset, sexual dysfunction, insomnia/sedation, SIADH, serotonin syndrome (with MAOIs/lithium/triptans). Withdrawal with sudden stop (paroxetine worst).
- **SNRIs (venlafaxine, duloxetine, desvenlafaxine, levomilnacipran):** Nausea, HTN (venlafaxine high dose), sexual dysfunction, withdrawal.
- **Bupropion:** No sexual side effects, no weight gain. Increases energy. Seizure risk (avoid in eating disorders, TBI).
- **Mirtazapine:** Weight gain + sedation (good for elderly with insomnia/poor appetite).
- **TCAs (amitriptyline, nortriptyline, imipramine, desipramine):** Anticholinergic (dry mouth, constipation, urinary retention, blurred vision), sedation, cardiac toxicity (prolonged QTc). Nortriptyline less anticholinergic.
- **MAOIs (phenelzine, tranylcypromine, selegiline):** Dietary restrictions (tyramine: aged cheese, wine, cured meats -> hypertensive crisis). Drug interactions. Reserved for refractory.

**Antipsychotics:**
- **First-gen (haloperidol, chlorpromazine, fluphenazine):** EPS (dystonia, akathisia, parkinsonism, tardive dyskinesia), hyperprolactinemia, NMS (neuroleptic malignant syndrome: fever, rigidity, autonomic instability, elevated CK, treat with dantrolene, bromocriptine), QTc prolongation.
- **Second-gen (olanzapine, risperidone, quetiapine, aripiprazole, ziprasidone, paliperidone, lurasidone, clozapine):** Less EPS generally. Weight gain/metabolic syndrome (clozapine, olanzapine worst). QTc prolongation (ziprasidone, iloperidone). Agranulocytosis (clozapine - weekly ANC monitoring). Clozapine for treatment-resistant schizophrenia.

**Anticoagulants:**
- **Warfarin:** Vitamin K antagonist. Monitor INR (2-3 for most, 2.5-3.5 for mechanical mitral valve). Reversal: vitamin K, FFP, PCC. Drug/food interactions (CYP2C9, VKORC1).
- **Heparin (UFH):** Activates antithrombin III. Monitor aPTT. Reversal: protamine. Short half-life.
- **LMWH (enoxaparin, dalteparin):** More predictable. Monitor anti-Xa if needed (obesity, renal failure, pregnancy). Reversal: protamine (partial).
- **DOACs (rivaroxaban, apixaban, edoxaban, dabigatran):** Direct factor Xa (rivaroxaban, apixaban, edoxaban) or direct thrombin (dabigatran) inhibitors. Fixed dosing, no routine monitoring. Reversal: andexanet alfa (Xa inhibitors), idarucizumab (dabigatran). Caution in renal/hepatic impairment.

**Lipid-Lowering Agents:**
- **Statins (atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin, pravastatin):** HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors. Reduce LDL, CRP. Side effects: myopathy/myositis/rhabdomyolysis (rare), transaminitis, increased risk of DM (minor). High-intensity: atorvastatin 40-80 mg, rosuvastatin 20-40 mg.
- **Ezetimibe:** Blocks intestinal cholesterol absorption. Add-on to statin.
- **PCSK9i (evolocumab, alirocumab):** Monoclonal antibodies, dramatically lower LDL. SC injection q2-4 weeks. For FH, statin intolerance, very high risk + not at goal.
- **Fibrates (fenofibrate, gemfibrozil):** Lower triglycerides, raise HDL. Avoid with statins (gemfibrozil increases myopathy risk).

### 4.2 Surgery

**Approaches:**
- **Open surgery:** Traditional incision. Full visualization, direct access. More pain, longer recovery, larger scar, higher infection risk.
- **Laparoscopic (minimally invasive):** Small incisions (5-12 mm), camera + instruments, CO2 insufflation. Less pain, shorter hospital stay, faster recovery, smaller scars. Used for cholecystectomy, appendectomy, colectomy, hernia repair, bariatric, nephrectomy, splenectomy.
- **Robotic surgery:** Da Vinci system. Enhanced 3D visualization, wristed instruments, tremor filtration, ergonomics. Used for prostatectomy, hysterectomy, cardiac surgery, thoracic, complex oncology.
- **Endoscopic:** Through natural orifices or small incisions (NOTES). EGD, colonoscopy, bronchoscopy, cystoscopy, arthroscopy.
- **Endovascular:** Via blood vessels. Stenting (coronary, carotid, renal, peripheral), EVAR (endovascular aneurysm repair), TAVR (aortic valve replacement), TACE. Less invasive than open.

**Common Procedures:**
- **Appendectomy:** Laparoscopic preferred. For appendicitis. Pre-op antibiotics.
- **Cholecystectomy:** Laparoscopic. For cholelithiasis, cholecystitis.
- **Colectomy:** Open or laparoscopic. For colon cancer, diverticulitis, IBD.
- **Hernia Repair:** Inguinal, umbilical, incisional, hiatal. Mesh (open or laparoscopic).
- **Prostatectomy:** Radical for cancer (open, laparoscopic, robotic). TURP for BPH.
- **CABG:** For multivessel CAD, left main, diabetic. LIMA to LAD preferred. Vein grafts.
- **Joint Replacement:** Hip (THA) and knee (TKA). For severe OA. Components: metal + polyethylene +/or ceramic. Cemented or uncemented.
- **Craniotomy:** For tumor, aneurysm, hematoma, AVM. Stereotactic navigation.
- **Spinal Surgery:** Discectomy, laminectomy, fusion, kyphoplasty. For herniated disc, stenosis, fracture, deformity, tumor.

### 4.3 Radiation Therapy

**Definition:** High-energy radiation to kill cancer cells. Local treatment. Works by damaging DNA (directly or via free radicals). Fractionation exploits differences in DNA repair between cancer and normal tissue.

**Types:** External beam (EBRT - most common): 3D conformal, IMRT (intensity-modulated), VMAT, SRS (stereotactic radiosurgery: Gamma Knife, CyberKnife - brain/spine), SBRT (stereotactic body RT - lung, liver, pancreas, spine). Brachytherapy: radioactive source placed inside or near tumor (prostate, cervix, breast). Systemic: radioisotopes (I-131 for thyroid, Lu-177 for neuroendocrine/prostate).

**Side Effects:** Acute (during treatment): fatigue, dermatitis, mucositis, esophagitis, diarrhea, cystitis, alopecia (site-dependent). Late (months-years): fibrosis, xerostomia, pneumonitis, enteritis, cystitis, cognitive impairment (brain), secondary malignancy, cardiotoxicity (breast/lung), hypothyroidism (neck).

### 4.4 Physical Therapy & Rehabilitation

**Goals:** Pain reduction, restoration of function/mobility, strength/endurance, balance/coordination, prevention of falls/injury.

**Modalities:** Therapeutic exercise (strengthening, stretching, aerobic, balance), Manual therapy (massage, mobilization, manipulation), Modalities (heat/cold, ultrasound, TENS, IFC), Gait training (with/without assistive devices), Balance/proprioception training, Postural correction, Occupational therapy (ADLs, fine motor, splinting, adaptive equipment), Speech therapy (swallowing, communication), Cardiac rehabilitation (post-MI, HF), Pulmonary rehabilitation (COPD).

### 4.5 Alternative Medicine

**Acupuncture:** Traditional Chinese Medicine - needles at meridian points. Evidence for pain (chronic pain, OA, headache, post-op), nausea/vomiting (post-chemotherapy, post-op). Mechanism: endorphin release, gate control, neuromodulation.

**Herbal Medicine:** Varied evidence. St. Johns Wort (depression - interacts with many drugs), Echinacea (common cold - limited evidence), Ginkgo biloba (dementia - limited/no effect), Saw palmetto (BPH - limited), Glucosamine/chondroitin (OA - no strong evidence), Turmeric/curcumin (inflammation - limited), Melatonin (sleep, jet lag - effective).

**Chiropractic:** Spinal manipulation for musculoskeletal conditions (especially low back pain - moderate evidence). Risks: stroke from cervical manipulation (rare, vertebral artery dissection). Contraindications: osteoporosis, fracture, tumor, infection, cord compression.

### 4.6 Emergency Medicine Approach

**ABCDE Approach:**
- **A - Airway:** Assess patency. Chin lift/jaw thrust. Suction. Oropharyngeal/nasopharyngeal airway. Definitive airway (ET intubation, cricothyroidotomy if cannot intubate). Protect C-spine if trauma.
- **B - Breathing:** Assess respiratory rate, effort, depth, symmetry, O2 saturation. Listen for breath sounds. Give O2 (target SpO2 >=94-98% or 88-92% if COPD). Bag-valve-mask ventilation. Needle decompression for tension pneumothorax. Chest tube for hemothorax/pneumothorax.
- **C - Circulation:** Assess pulse, BP, skin color/temperature, capillary refill, mental status. IV access x2 large bore. Start IV fluids (crystalloids: NS or LR, blood if hemorrhagic shock). Control external bleeding (direct pressure, tourniquet, pressure dressing). Vasopressors (norepinephrine) if shock persists. CPR if no pulse. Defibrillation for VF/pulseless VT.
- **D - Disability:** Neurologic exam: AVPU (Alert, Verbal, Pain, Unresponsive) or GCS. Pupils. Check glucose (treat hypoglycemia). Assess for stroke, seizure, intoxication.
- **E - Exposure/Environment:** Full exam (log roll for trauma). Remove clothing. Maintain body temperature (warm blankets, warm fluids). Cover patient. Check for hidden injuries.

**ACLS (Advanced Cardiac Life Support):**
- **VF/Pulseless VT:** Defibrillate (biphasic 120-200 J) -> CPR 2 min -> check rhythm -> defib again -> CPR -> amiodarone 300 mg (then 150 mg) or lidocaine 1-1.5 mg/kg -> treat reversible causes (Hs: Hypoxia, Hypovolemia, Hypo/Hyperkalemia, Hypothermia, Hydrogen ion/acidosis; Ts: Tension pneumothorax, Tamponade (cardiac), Toxins, Thrombosis (coronary/pulmonary)).
- **Pulseless Electrical Activity (PEA)/Asystole:** CPR -> epinephrine 1 mg q3-5 min -> identify/treat cause -> no defibrillation.
- **Bradycardia:** Symptomatic -> atropine 0.5 mg q3-5 min (max 3 mg) -> transcutaneous pacing -> transvenous pacing or dopamine/epinephrine infusion.
- **Tachycardia (stable):** Narrow QRS: vagal maneuvers -> adenosine -> BB/CCB. Wide QRS regular: amiodarone or procainamide or cardioversion.
- **Tachycardia (unstable):** Synchronized cardioversion (narrow: 50-100J, wide: 100-200J monophasic).

**Trauma:** ATLS protocol. Primary survey (ABCDE) -> resuscitation -> secondary survey (head-to-toe exam + imaging) -> definitive care. E-FAST for free fluid (FAST: Morrison pouch, splenorenal, pelvic, pericardial). CXR, pelvic XR, C-spine XR/CT. Massive transfusion protocol (1:1:1 ratio PRBC:FFP:platelets for hemorrhagic shock). Damage control surgery (control hemorrhage, contamination, temporary closure, ICU resuscitation -> definitive repair).

## 6. Medical Emergencies

#### Cardiac Arrest
**Definition:** Sudden cessation of cardiac output. Etiology: VF/VT (most common initially), asystole, PEA. Causes: Ischemic heart disease (most common), cardiomyopathy, valvular disease, electrolyte abnormalities, drug toxicity, hypothermia, PE, tension pneumothorax, cardiac tamponade, hemorrhage, hypoxia.

**Treatment:** Immediate CPR (30:2 compressions:ventilations, rate 100-120/min, depth 5-6 cm, complete chest recoil, minimize interruptions). Defibrillate VF/pulseless VT as soon as available. Advanced airway (ETT or supraglottic) with continuous compressions. IV/IO access. Epinephrine 1 mg q3-5 min. Amiodarone 300 mg then 150 mg for refractory VF/VT. Treat reversible causes (Hs and Ts). Post-resuscitation care: targeted temperature management (TTM, 32-36C x 24 hrs), PCI for STEMI, intensive care.

#### Anaphylaxis
**Definition:** Severe, life-threatening, generalized hypersensitivity reaction. Onset minutes to hours. Triggers: food (peanuts, tree nuts, shellfish, eggs, milk), medications (penicillin, NSAIDs, contrast), insect stings (Hymenoptera: bees, wasps), latex.

**Symptoms:** Urticaria, angioedema (face, lips, tongue, throat), stridor, wheezing, dyspnea, hypotension, tachycardia, nausea/vomiting, abdominal pain, dizziness, syncope, feeling of impending doom.

**Treatment:** IM epinephrine (0.3-0.5 mg 1:1000, anterolateral thigh, repeat q5-15 min) - first and most important. Place supine, elevate legs. O2, IV fluids. H1 blocker (diphenhydramine 25-50 mg). H2 blocker (famotidine 20 mg). Methylprednisolone 125 mg. Albuterol for bronchospasm. Glucagon if patient on beta-blocker. Observe for biphasic reaction (up to 20% within 4-8 hrs). Epinephrine auto-injector prescription on discharge.

#### Status Epilepticus
**Definition:** Seizure >5 min or recurrent without recovery between. Causes: anticonvulsant non-compliance, stroke, tumor, infection, trauma, metabolic (hypoglycemia, hyponatremia, hypocalcemia), alcohol/drug withdrawal, CNS infection.

**Treatment:** Stabilize (ABCs, O2, check glucose). Lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg IV (max 4 mg/dose) or midazolam 10 mg IM. Repeat if no response after 5 min. If still seizing: fosphenytoin 20 mg/kg PE IV or valproate 20-40 mg/kg IV or levetiracetam 60 mg/kg IV. If refractory: intubate + continuous IV anesthetic (midazolam, propofol, pentobarbital) with EEG monitoring. Identify/treat underlying cause.

#### Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA)
**Definition:** Hyperglycemia (>250), metabolic acidosis (pH<7.3, HCO3<15), ketosis. Precipitants: infection, missed insulin, MI, stroke, pancreatitis, steroids, surgery.

**Treatment:** IVF (NS 1L over 1 hr, then 250-500 mL/hr). IV insulin (0.1 U/kg bolus + 0.1 U/kg/hr infusion). When glucose ~200-250, switch to D5-10 1/2NS. Monitor K+: replete if K+<5.3 (20-40 mEq/L). Replace bicarbonate not routinely (only if pH<6.9). Correct other electrolytes. Identify/treat precipitant. Transition to SC insulin when anion gap closed, patient eating, overlap 2-4 hrs.

#### Hyperglycemic Hyperosmolar State (HHS)
**Definition:** Extreme hyperglycemia (>600), severe dehydration, hyperosmolality (>320), no/minimal ketosis. Typically T2D, older patients.

**Treatment:** Aggressive IVF (NS 1-2L over 1-2 hrs, then 0.45% NS 250-500 mL/hr). Insulin lower dose (0.05 U/kg/hr, bolus may not be needed). Correct K+. Monitor for complications: thrombosis (prophylactic heparin), cerebral edema, rhabdomyolysis.

#### Hypoglycemia
**Definition:** Blood glucose <70 mg/dL. Causes: insulin/SU excess, missed meals, excess exercise, alcohol, liver/kidney failure. Symptoms: autonomic (tremor, sweating, palpitations, hunger, anxiety), neuroglycopenic (confusion, drowsiness, slurred speech, seizure, coma).

**Treatment:** Conscious, able to swallow: 15-20g fast-acting glucose (glucose tablets 3-4, 4 oz juice/soda, 1 Tbsp honey). Recheck in 15 min. If not improved, repeat. Unconscious: IM glucagon 1 mg (adults) or IV D50 (25g, 1 amp). Once conscious, give complex carbohydrate. Identify/treat cause.

#### Acute Respiratory Failure
**Definition:** PaO2 <60 or PaCO2 >50 + pH <7.30. Type 1 (hypoxemic: pneumonia, PE, ARDS, pulmonary edema, ILD). Type 2 (hypercapnic: COPD exacerbation, OHS, NM disease, opioid overdose, asthma).

**Treatment:** O2 (goal SpO2 88-92% type 2, >94% type 1). NIV (BiPAP/CPAP) for type 2 (COPD, pulmonary edema). HFNC (high flow nasal cannula) for hypoxemic failure. Intubation + mechanical ventilation (lung-protective: TV 6-8 mL/kg PBW, plateau pressure <30). Treat underlying cause.

#### ARDS (Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome)
**Definition:** Acute onset hypoxemic respiratory failure from inflammatory pulmonary edema. Causes: sepsis, pneumonia, aspiration, trauma, pancreatitis, transfusion, burns, drug overdose. Berlin Criteria: acute (<1 wk), bilateral opacities on CXR/CT, not fully explained by effusion/atelectasis/nodules, PaO2/FiO2 <=300 (mild 200-300, moderate 100-200, severe <100 with PEEP >=5).

**Treatment:** Lung-protective ventilation (TV 6 mL/kg PBW, plateau <30, driving pressure <15). PEEP. Prone positioning (severe, improves oxygenation and mortality). Neuromuscular blockade (cisatracurium, early severe). Conservative fluid strategy. ECMO for refractory. Treat underlying cause.

#### Hemorrhagic Shock
**Definition:** Hemorrhage leading to inadequate tissue perfusion. Classes: I (<15% loss), II (15-30%, tachycardia, narrow pulse pressure), III (30-40%, hypotension, tachycardia, altered MS, oliguria), IV (>40%, profound hypotension, anuria, unconscious).

**Treatment:** Direct pressure (not tourniquet unless extremity hemorrhage uncontrolled). IV access x2 large bore. Start blood products (1:1:1 PRBC:FFP:platelets). Massive transfusion protocol. Limit crystalloid (avoid dilutional coagulopathy). TXA (tranexamic acid 1g over 10 min then 1g over 8 hrs, within 3 hrs of injury). Early surgical/IR intervention. Damage control resuscitation. Reversal of anticoagulation if applicable. Goal: SBP 80-90 (permissive hypotension until surgical hemostasis). Warm patient, avoid hypothermia, acidosis, hypocalcemia (citrate in blood products).

#### Acute Abdomen
**Definition:** Severe, acute-onset abdominal pain requiring urgent surgical evaluation. Causes: appendicitis, cholecystitis, perforated peptic ulcer, pancreatitis, diverticulitis, bowel obstruction, mesenteric ischemia, ruptured AAA, ectopic pregnancy, ovarian torsion, IBD, trauma, gastroenteritis (but medical).

**Diagnosis:** History (location, onset, character, radiation, duration, progression, aggravating/relieving factors, associated symptoms). Physical exam (localized vs diffuse tenderness, guarding, rigidity, rebound, distension, bowel sounds, hernias, scars, masses, rectal/pelvic exam). Labs (CBC, BMP, LFTs, lipase, lactate, HCG, UA). Imaging (upright CXR for free air, CT abdomen/pelvis with IV contrast preferred, US for RUQ/gallbladder/pelvis).

**Surgical Indications:** Peritonitis (rigidity, rebound, guarding), perforation (free air), obstruction (complete, closed-loop, strangulation), ischemia, uncontrolled hemorrhage, ruptured viscous, necrotizing infection. Consult general surgery.

#### Sepsis and Septic Shock
**Definition:** Sepsis = life-threatening organ dysfunction from infection (qSOFA >=2 or SOFA score >=2). Septic shock = sepsis + vasopressor requirement + lactate >2.

**Treatment:** Hour-1 bundle: measure lactate, obtain blood cultures before antibiotics, broad-spectrum antibiotics, crystalloid 30 mL/kg for hypotension/lactate >=4, vasopressors if still hypotensive (norepinephrine first-line, add vasopressin if needed). Source control (drain abscess, remove infected catheter, surgical debridement). Reassess fluids, lactate, perfusion.

#### Pulmonary Embolism (Massive)
**Definition:** PE causing hemodynamic instability (SBP <90 or drop >40 for >15 min, cardiac arrest, obstructive shock). High-risk PE (massive).

**Treatment:** Immediate anticoagulation (heparin or LMWH). Thrombolysis (alteplase 100 mg over 2 hrs or 0.6 mg/kg over 15 min for cardiac arrest) if no contraindications. Surgical embolectomy or catheter-directed therapy if thrombolysis contraindicated/fails. ECMO if refractory. Intermediate-high risk (submassive - RV dysfunction on echo, elevated troponin, but normotensive): consider thrombolysis if low bleeding risk but evidence mixed. Anticoagulation alone sufficient for most non-massive PE.

#### Tension Pneumothorax
**Definition:** Air trapped in pleural space with one-way valve effect, causing mediastinal shift, decreased venous return, hypotension. Causes: trauma (often with positive pressure ventilation), spontaneous, iatrogenic.

**Symptoms:** Respiratory distress, chest pain, tracheal deviation away from side, hyperresonance, decreased breath sounds, distended neck veins, hypotension, cyanosis, cardiac arrest.

**Treatment:** Immediate decompression: large bore needle (14-16G, 2nd intercostal space midclavicular line or 5th ICS anterior axillary line). Then tube thoracostomy (chest tube, 4th-5th ICS midaxillary). Do not wait for X-ray if suspected tension.

#### Cardiac Tamponade
**Definition:** Fluid in pericardial sac compressing heart, decreasing cardiac output. Beck triad: hypotension, muffled heart sounds, distended neck veins. Causes: trauma (penetrating), post-cardiac surgery, malignancy, uremia, pericarditis, aortic dissection, TB, iatrogenic (post-procedure).

**Diagnosis:** Echo (pericardial effusion, RV collapse, RA collapse, IVC dilatation without respiratory collapse, respiratory variation in mitral/tricuspid inflow). ECG: low voltage, electrical alternans. CXR: enlarged cardiac silhouette (water bottle heart).

**Treatment:** Emergency pericardiocentesis (subxiphoid or parasternal approach, echo or fluoro-guided, drain as much as possible). Surgical pericardial window if recurrent or for large effusions.

#### Thermal Burns
**Classification:** 1st degree (epidermis only, erythema, pain). 2nd degree superficial (papillary dermis, blisters, very painful). 2nd degree deep (reticular dermis, blanchable or not, less painful). 3rd degree (full thickness, all dermis, painless, white/charred/leathery, no capillary refill). 4th degree (extends to muscle/bone).

**Assessment:** Rule of 9s (head/neck 9%, each arm 9%, anterior trunk 18%, posterior trunk 18%, each leg 18%, perineum 1%) or palmar method (patient palm = ~1% each). Criteria for burn center referral: >10% 2nd degree, 3rd degree any, hands/face/feet/genitalia/perineum/over joints, chemical/electrical/inhalation, circumferential, extremes of age, comorbidities.

**Treatment:** Stop burning process (remove clothing, cool with room temperature water). Airway assessment (inhalation injury: closed space fire, facial burns, singed nasal hair, carbonaceous sputum, hoarseness, stridor - intubate early). Breathing (escharotomy for circumferential chest burns restricting ventilation). Circulation (escharotomy for circumferential extremity burns). Aggressive IVF (Parkland formula: 4 mL/kg/%TBSA in first 24 hrs, half in first 8 hrs, half in next 16 hrs). Wound care (silver sulfadiazine, mafenide acetate, bacitracin, biosynthetic dressings). Pain control. Nutrition (high protein, high calorie). Infection prevention (topical antimicrobials). Tetanus prophylaxis.

#### Rhabdomyolysis
**Definition:** Muscle breakdown releasing intracellular contents (CK, myoglobin, K, PO4) into circulation. Causes: crush injury, seizures, strenuous exercise, statins, drugs (cocaine, amphetamines, alcohol), infections, heat stroke, compartment syndrome, malignant hyperthermia, NMS.

**Symptoms:** Muscle pain, weakness, dark urine (tea/cola colored). Labs: elevated CK (>5x ULN or >5000 U/L, peaks 1-3 days post-injury), hyperkalemia, hyperphosphatemia, hypocalcemia (early), hypercalcemia (later), elevated Cr, myoglobinuria.

**Complications:** AKI (from myoglobin cast nephropathy, vasoconstriction, direct tubular toxicity), compartment syndrome, DIC, cardiac arrhythmias (hyperkalemia, hypocalcemia).

**Treatment:** Aggressive IVF (NS 200-500 mL/hr to maintain urine output >200 mL/hr). Correct electrolyte abnormalities (especially K+). Consider bicarbonate (alkalinizes urine, may reduce myoglobin cast formation - controversial). Mannitol (controversial). Dialysis for refractory hyperkalemia/acidosis/volume overload. Treat compartment syndrome (fasciotomy).

#### Compartment Syndrome
**Definition:** Increased pressure within a closed fascial compartment compromising perfusion. Causes: fracture, crush, hemorrhage, reperfusion, tight cast/splint, burns.

**Symptoms (5 Ps):** Pain out of proportion (earliest, often first sign), Paresthesia (later), Pallor, Pulselessness (very late - muscle necrosis already occurred), Paralysis (late). Pain on passive stretch of involved muscles is most sensitive early sign.

**Diagnosis:** Clinical. Compartment pressure measurement: absolute >30 mmHg or delta pressure (diastolic BP - compartment pressure) <30 mmHg.

**Treatment:** Emergency fasciotomy (remove all constricting dressings/casts first). Full-length skin incision and release of all involved compartments. Necrotic muscle debridement. Delayed closure. Complications: Volkmann ischemic contracture, renal failure, amputation, death.

### 7.5 Important Drug Interactions

- **Warfarin + many drugs:** CYP2C9, VKORC1 interactions (antibiotics, antifungals, amiodarone, NSAIDs, acetaminophen >2g/day, cranberry juice, green leafy vegetables). Monitor INR.
- **Statins + CYP3A4 inhibitors:** Macrolides, azole antifungals, cyclosporine, grapefruit juice increase myopathy/rhabdomyolysis risk.
- **ACEi + ARB + MRA + K+ supplements:** Dangerous hyperkalemia.
- **SSRI + MAOI:** Serotonin syndrome (hyperthermia, rigidity, autonomic instability, altered mental status).
- **SSRI + triptans:** Serotonin syndrome risk.
- **B-lactam antibiotics + methotrexate:** Reduced MTX clearance -> toxicity.
- **Fluoroquinolones + NSAIDs:** Increased CNS stimulation, seizure risk.
- **TMP-SMX + warfarin:** Increased INR (TMP inhibits CYP2C9).
- **Amiodarone + many drugs:** CYP450 inhibition -> increased levels of warfarin, statins, digoxin, antiarrhythmics.
- **Clopidogrel + PPIs (omeprazole):** Reduced clopidogrel activation (CYP2C19 interaction).
- **Methotrexate + TMP-SMX:** Severe marrow suppression (additive antifolate).
- **Linezolid + SSRIs/SNRIs:** Serotonin syndrome (MAOI activity).

### 7.6 Contraindications & Cautions Summary

- **ACEi:** Pregnancy, angioedema history, bilateral renal artery stenosis.
- **NSAlDs:** CKD, GI bleeding/ulcer history, ASA allergy, severe CAD (caution), HF, anticoagulation.
- **Metformin:** eGFR <30, acute illness with lactic acidosis risk (sepsis, liver failure, HF exacerbation, MI, dehydration).
- **Tetracyclines/FQs:** Children <8 (tooth discoloration, cartilage damage).
- **Aminoglycosides:** Renal impairment, hearing loss, myasthenia gravis (neuromuscular blockade), pregnancy.
- **Warfarin:** Pregnancy (1st trimester), bleeding disorders, uncontrolled HTN, recent surgery.
- **Estrogen-containing OCPs:** Smokers >35, HTN, migraine with aura, h/o VTE, breast cancer.
- **Lithium:** Renal disease (monitor), hypothyroidism, dehydration (toxicity risk).
- **Clozapine:** ANC <1500, severe CNS depression, paralytic ileus, uncontrolled epilepsy.
- **Statins:** Active liver disease, pregnancy, breastfeeding.

### 7.7 Therapeutic Drug Monitoring (TDM)

**Drugs requiring TDM:**
- **Vancomycin:** Trough 15-20 mcg/mL (serious infections), 10-15 (uncomplicated). AUC/MIC ratio >400.

## 8. Medical Procedures

#### Endotracheal Intubation
**Indications:** Airway protection (GCS <8, loss of reflexes), respiratory failure (hypoxemic/hypercapnic), airway obstruction, anticipated deterioration, general anesthesia.

**Equipment:** Laryngoscope (Macintosh curved or Miller straight blade), ETT (7.0-8.0 mm ID female, 8.0-9.0 male), stylet, syringe for cuff inflation, suction, bag-valve-mask, O2 source, confirmation device (capnography, colorimetric ETCO2), securing tape/holder, sedation (etomidate, propofol, ketamine), paralytic (succinylcholine, rocuronium).

**Technique:** Preoxygenate (100% O2 for 3 min or 8 vital capacity breaths). Position (sniffing morning air, ear to sternal notch). Open mouth, sweep tongue left. Insert blade midline or from right, advance to vallecula (Macintosh) or lift epiglottis (Miller). Lift (do NOT pry on teeth) to visualize glottic opening. Insert ETT through cords, inflate cuff. Confirm placement (capnography: end-tidal CO2, bilateral breath sounds, no epigastric sounds, chest rise, fogging, CO2 color change). Secure tube. Check depth (21-23 cm at incisors for men, 20-22 for women). CXR for position (tip 2-4 cm above carina). Difficult airway: anticipate (LEMON: Look externally, Evaluate 3-3-2, Mallampati, Obstruction, Neck mobility). Have rescue devices (LMA, video laryngoscope, bougie, cricothyroidotomy kit, surgical airway).

#### Central Venous Line (CVC) Placement
**Indications:** Central access for drugs (vasopressors, TPN, chemotherapy), hemodynamic monitoring (CVP, ScvO2), inadequate peripheral IV, dialysis, transvenous pacing, repeated blood draws.

**Sites:** Internal jugular (IJ - preferred for access/emergency), Subclavian (lower infection risk, cannot compress if bleeding), Femoral (highest infection/DVT risk, compressible, use when IJ/SC not accessible).

**Technique (Seldinger):** Ultrasound guidance (decreases complications). Position patient (Trendelenburg for IJ/subclavian to distend veins). Sterile prep, drape, PPE. Locate vein with US (IJ: lateral to carotid artery). Local anesthesia. Access with introducer needle on US guidance. Confirm venous blood (dark, non-pulsatile, low pressure). Pass J-wire through needle (should pass smoothly). Remove needle. Make small incision at wire entry. Pass dilator over wire (advance gently, do not force, risk of vessel perforation). Remove dilator, thread catheter over wire. Remove wire. Confirm position (attach transducer, check venous waveform, blood gas: venous). Flush and lock each lumen. Suture in place. Sterile dressing. CXR to confirm tip location (superior vena cava, above right atrium) and exclude pneumothorax (subclavian/IJ).

**Complications:** Pneumothorax (subclavian/IJ), arterial puncture (carotid, subclavian), catheter-related bloodstream infection, air embolism, venous thrombosis, hematoma, hemothorax, arrhythmia (wire in right ventricle), cardiac tamponade (perforation), nerve injury, thoracic duct injury (left IJ/subclavian).

#### Chest Tube (Tube Thoracostomy)
**Indications:** Pneumothorax (large, tension, on positive pressure, traumatic), hemothorax, pleural effusion/empyema, chylothorax, post-operative (cardiac, thoracic).

**Technique:** Position (supine with arm behind head for midaxillary approach, or slightly upright). Identify insertion site (4th-5th intercostal space, midaxillary line - triangle of safety: anterior to latissimus dorsi, posterior to pectoralis major, above the diaphragm). Sterile prep, drape. Local anesthesia (lidocaine 1-2% with epinephrine, infiltrate skin, subcutaneous, rib periosteum, pleura). Small incision (2-3 cm). Blunt dissection with clamp through subcutaneous tissue and intercostal muscles (just above rib margin to avoid neurovascular bundle). Puncture pleura with clamp tip (feel/pop, air or fluid escape). Insert finger to confirm correct intrapleural position. Grasp chest tube (24-32 Fr for pneumothorax/hemothorax/empyema, 20-24 Fr for simple pneumothorax, larger for hemothorax) with clamp at tip. Insert tube (direct posterior/apical for pneumothorax, posterior/inferior for effusion/hemothorax). Advance all drainage holes into pleura. Connect to drainage system (water seal, suction -20 cm H2O initially). Suture to secure. Purse-string suture at site (for closure when removed). Sterile dressing. CXR to confirm position and resolution.

**Complications:** Malposition (too far, too shallow, extrapleural, in fissure, kinked), hemorrhage (intercostal artery laceration - can be life-threatening, especially in elderly with tortuous arteries), infection/empyema, lung laceration, re-expansion pulmonary edema, subcutaneous emphysema, diaphragmatic or intra-abdominal injury (spleen, liver, stomach, colon - especially if placed too low), nerve injury (long thoracic, phrenic, vagus), retained hemothorax/ pneumothorax, persistent air leak. Removal: when lung expanded, no air leak, minimal output (<200-300 mL/day for effusion, <100-150 mL/day if hemothorax). Remove on inspiration or at end-expiration during Valsalva. Suture site closed. CXR post-removal.

#### Lumbar Puncture (Spinal Tap)
**Indications:** CSF analysis (meningitis/encephalitis, SAH, demyelinating disease, carcinomatous meningitis, Guillain-Barre, normal pressure hydrocephalus). CSF pressure measurement (IIH/pseudotumor cerebri). Intrathecal therapy (chemotherapy, antibiotics, pain management).

**Contraindications:** Increased ICP (mass lesion, obstruction - risk of herniation). Perform CT before LP if concern (focal neurologic signs, papilledema, altered mental status, new-onset seizure, age >60, immunosuppression). Coagulopathy (platelets <50, INR >1.4, therapeutic anticoagulation). Infection at puncture site. Spinal cord compression. Severe spinal deformity.

**Technique:** Position (lateral decubitus, knees to chest, chin to chest, back perpendicular to bed; or sitting, leaning forward). Identify L3-L4 or L4-L5 interspace (line between iliac crests crosses L4 spinous process or interspace). Sterile prep and drape. Local anesthesia (lidocaine 1-2%, raise skin wheal, infiltrate deeper). Insert spinal needle (22-25 gauge) midline, angled slightly cephalad (toward umbilicus). Advance slowly. Remove stylet periodically (every few mm after initial 3-4 cm). Feel for pop (ligamentum flavum). CSF flow confirms entry. If bone hit, withdraw to skin, redirect. Measure opening pressure (normal 10-20 cm H2O, lateral position; up to 25 cm if obese; <6 suggests low pressure; >25 elevated). Collect CSF into sterile tubes (tube 1: chemistry/protein/glucose; tube 2: microbiology/Gram stain/culture; tube 3: cell count/differential; tube 4: special studies/cytology/PCR, tube 5: serology/others). Replace stylet before removing needle. Patient flat for 30-60 min (controversial, but lying may reduce post-LP headache risk). Apply bandage.

**Complications:** Post-dural puncture headache (most common, 10-30%, often positional, worse sitting/standing, relieved lying down. Risk lower with smaller gauge, atraumatic needle. Treatment: hydration, caffeine, analgesics, blood patch). Bleeding (spinal epidural hematoma - rare but emergent). Infection (meningitis - rare with proper sterile technique). Herniation (if increased ICP). Nerve root irritation (transient paresthesia). CSF leak.

#### Paracentesis
**Indications:** Diagnostic (new ascites evaluation, suspected SBP: PMN count >=250). Therapeutic (tense ascites causing respiratory distress, pain, or discomfort). LVP (large volume paracentesis, >5L removed).

**Contraindications:** Coagulopathy (relative: need prophylactic platelets/FFP if severe). Pregnancy (suprapubic approach). Distended bowel in path. Abdominal wall infection (choose different site).

**Technique:** Patient supine or slightly sitting up. Identify puncture site (left lower quadrant, lateral to rectus sheath, or midline infra-umbilical, or ultrasound-guided to find pocket of fluid). Sterile prep, drape. Local anesthesia. Insert needle/catheter (18-22 gauge) using Z-track (to minimize leakage). Advance until fluid returns. Collect fluid (cell count, albumin, total protein, culture/Gram stain, cytology, glucose, LDH, amylase, bilirubin, triglycerides, TB PCR/culture - as clinically indicated). For LVP: attach drainage tubing/bag, drain until no longer flows or up to 10L safely (give albumin 6-8g per liter if >5L removed to prevent post-paracentesis circulatory dysfunction). Remove needle/catheter, apply pressure. Bandage.

**Complications:** Bleeding (abdominal wall hematoma, hemoperitoneum - usually from inferior epigastric artery or superficial epigastric artery injury, risk with coagulopathy, prior surgical scars), bowel perforation (rare with proper technique and US guidance), bladder puncture (empty bladder first!), persistent ascitic fluid leak (use Z-track), infection (peritonitis), hypotension (post-LVP if no albumin given), vasovagal reaction.

#### Thoracentesis
**Indications:** Diagnostic (pleural effusion workup). Therapeutic (large effusion causing respiratory compromise, dyspnea).

**Contraindications:** Coagulopathy (relative). High risk: small effusion (<1 cm), positive pressure ventilation (increases pneumothorax risk). Infection over site. Anticoagulation.

**Technique:** Patient sitting upright, leaning forward over table, arms supported. Identify site by US (posterior, above rib to avoid neurovascular bundle, in area with deepest fluid). Sterile prep, drape. Local anesthesia (infiltrate skin, subcutaneous, intercostal muscles, parietal pleura - aspirate pleural fluid to confirm). Insert needle over superior rib border. Collect fluid. Remove no more than 1-1.5L for therapeutic (risk of re-expansion pulmonary edema if larger volume removed abruptly). Remove needle, apply bandage. CXR post-procedure to evaluate pneumothorax.

**Complications:** Pneumothorax (most common), hemothorax, re-expansion pulmonary edema, bleeding, infection, vasovagal response, liver/spleen puncture (if placed too low), cough (from lung re-expansion).

#### Joint Injection/Aspiration
**Indications:** Diagnostic (septic arthritis: cell count >50,000 WBC, Gram stain, culture; crystal arthropathy: gout [negatively birefringent monosodium urate crystals], pseudogout [positively birefringent calcium pyrophosphate crystals]). Therapeutic (steroid injection for inflammatory arthritis, OA, tendinopathy, bursitis).

**Technique:** Identify landmarks (knee: medial or lateral approach to patellofemoral joint; shoulder: posterior or anterior; hip: anterior or lateral; elbow: lateral; wrist: dorsal; ankle: anteromedial or anterolateral; MCP/PIP: dorsal). Sterile prep. Local anesthetic (ethyl chloride spray or lidocaine). Insert needle (18-22 gauge for aspiration, 22-25 for injection) into joint space. Aspirate fluid for analysis (cell count, crystals, culture, Gram stain). For injection: inject steroid (triamcinolone, methylprednisolone, betamethasone) +/- lidocaine into joint. Remove needle, apply pressure. Rest joint for 24-48 hrs.

**Complications:** Infection (septic arthritis - rare with proper sterile technique). Bleeding. Post-injection flare (crystal-induced from steroid). Tendon rupture (corticosteroid, not for weight-bearing tendons). Skin/soft tissue atrophy (superficial injection, depot effect). Cartilage damage (repeated injections, controversial).

#### Incision and Drainage (I&D)
**Indications:** Cutaneous abscess (purulent collection in skin/subcutaneous tissue). Furuncle, carbuncle, suppurative hidradenitis, infected cyst.

**Technique:** Identify area of fluctuance. Mark margins. Sterile prep. Local anesthesia (lidocaine 1-2% with epinephrine, field block or infiltrate around abscess; avoid injecting into center - poor effect, increased pain). Make incision with scalpel (#11 blade) at point of maximal fluctuance, following skin crease lines. Extend incision across full length of abscess. Express pus (gentle pressure, cotton swab). Break up loculations with hemostat. Obtain wound culture (if immunocompromised, severe infection, recurrent, or antibiotic treatment needed). Irrigate with saline. Pack wound loosely with iodoform or plain gauze (allows healing by secondary intention, prevents premature closure). Cover with absorptive dressing. Arrange follow-up for packing removal/change in 24-48 hours. Antibiotics (if: surrounding cellulitis >5 cm, immunocompromised, DM, MRSA risk, recurrent abscess, severe infection, prosthetic hardware/valve, febrile/systemic signs). NSAIDs/analgesics. Warm compresses 3-4 times daily.

**Complications:** Incomplete drainage (loculations, inadequate incision), recurrence, bleeding (rare, usually from inadequate hemostasis), damage to underlying structures (nerves, vessels - avoid deep probing), bacteremia/sepsis (transient from manipulation), fistula formation, scarring.

## 9. Global Health

### 9.1 Major Health Organizations

**World Health Organization (WHO):** UN specialized agency for public health. Headquarters: Geneva, Switzerland. 194 member states. Functions: setting health standards, monitoring health trends, coordinating pandemic response, disease eradication (smallpox 1980, polio near-eradicated), International Health Regulations (IHR), Global Health Observatory data, WHO Model List of Essential Medicines, Framework Convention on Tobacco Control.

**Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC):** US federal agency (Atlanta, GA). Functions: disease surveillance (MMWR), outbreak investigation, public health guidelines, immunization schedules, travel health recommendations, global health security, Emergency Operations Center, CDC Yellow Book for travel medicine.

**National Institutes of Health (NIH):** US medical research agency (Bethesda, MD). 27 institutes/centers (NCI, NHLBI, NIAID, NINDS, etc.). World's largest biomedical research funder. ClinicalTrials.gov registry. PubMed/MEDLINE. Conducts and funds basic, translational, clinical research.

**Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF / Doctors Without Borders):** Independent humanitarian medical organization. Nobel Peace Prize 1999. Operates in conflict zones, epidemics, natural disasters, refugee settings. Provides emergency medical care, surgical programs, vaccination campaigns, nutrition programs, HIV/TB/malaria treatment.

**UNICEF:** Children's health and nutrition programs worldwide. Vaccination campaigns, malnutrition treatment, breastfeeding promotion, WASH (water, sanitation, hygiene), education. Major procurer of vaccines for low-income countries.

**World Bank:** Health financing for developing countries. Disease Control Priorities project. Universal health coverage financing. Pandemic preparedness funding.

**GAVI (Global Alliance for Vaccines and Immunization):** Public-private partnership to increase vaccine access in low-income countries. Introduces new vaccines (pneumococcal, rotavirus, HPV, malaria). Cofinancing arrangements. Market shaping.

**The Global Fund:** Fights AIDS, TB, and malaria. Finances country-led programs. Performance-based funding. Largest international financier of these three diseases. Sixth Replenishment (2023-2025) raised ~$15.7 billion.

### 9.2 Epidemiology Basics

**Definition:** Study of distribution and determinants of health-related states in populations. Application to control health problems.

**Key Measures:**
- **Incidence:** Number of new cases in a population over a time period. Incidence rate = new cases / person-time at risk.
- **Prevalence:** Total number of existing cases in a population at a specific point (point prevalence) or period (period prevalence). Prevalence = (new + existing) / total population.
- **Mortality rate:** Deaths per population per time. Case fatality rate (CFR) = deaths / confirmed cases x 100. Crude mortality rate.
- **Morbidity rate:** Illness in a population.

**Study Designs:**
- **Observational:** Cross-sectional (snapshot at one time), Case-control (compare exposures in cases vs controls - retrospective, good for rare diseases), Cohort (follow exposed and unexposed groups forward in time - prospective, measures incidence, calculates relative risk), Ecological (population-level data).
- **Experimental:** Randomized controlled trial (RCT - gold standard, randomization minimizes confounding). Blinding (single, double, triple). Placebo control. Intention-to-treat analysis. Community trial.
- **Systematic Review/Meta-analysis:** Pool data from multiple studies.

**Bias:** Selection bias (how subjects selected), Information bias (measurement error, recall bias), Confounding (third variable associated with both exposure and outcome - control with randomization, stratification, multivariable analysis, matching).

**Measures of Association:**
- **Relative Risk (RR):** Incidence in exposed / incidence in unexposed. RR = 1 (no effect), >1 (risk factor), <1 (protective).
- **Odds Ratio (OR):** Odds of exposure in cases / odds of exposure in controls. Approximates RR for rare diseases. Used in case-control studies.
- **Attributable Risk (AR):** Incidence in exposed - incidence in unexposed. Excess risk due to exposure.
- **Number Needed to Treat (NNT):** 1 / absolute risk reduction. Number needed to treat to prevent one event.
- **Number Needed to Harm (NNH):** 1 / attributable risk. Number needed to expose to cause one harm event.

**Screening Test Metrics:**
- **Sensitivity:** True positives / (true positives + false negatives). Ability to detect disease.
- **Specificity:** True negatives / (true negatives + false positives). Ability to rule out disease in healthy.
- **Positive Predictive Value (PPV):** True positives / all positives. Probability that a positive test means disease. Depends on prevalence.
- **Negative Predictive Value (NPV):** True negatives / all negatives. Probability that a negative test means no disease.
- **Likelihood Ratio:** LR+ = sensitivity / (1 - specificity). LR- = (1 - sensitivity) / specificity. Used to update pre-test to post-test probability.

### 9.3 Global Disease Burden

**Top Causes of Death Worldwide (WHO, 2019):**
1. Ischemic heart disease (16% of total deaths)
2. Stroke (11%)
3. COPD (6%)
4. Lower respiratory infections (5%)
5. Neonatal conditions (5%)
6. Lung, trachea, bronchus cancers (4%)
7. Alzheimer and other dementias (4%)
8. Diarrheal diseases (3%)
9. Diabetes mellitus (3%)
10. Kidney diseases (3%)

**Low-Income Countries:** Communicable diseases dominate (lower respiratory infections, malaria, TB, HIV/AIDS, diarrheal diseases, neonatal conditions). Maternal mortality remains high. Malnutrition underlies many deaths.

**High-Income Countries:** Non-communicable diseases dominate (CVD, cancer, dementia, COPD, diabetes). Injuries (including drug overdose, suicide) significant. Mental health burden.

**Disability-Adjusted Life Year (DALY):** Years of life lost (YLL) + Years lived with disability (YLD). One DALY = one lost year of healthy life. Leading DALY causes: neonatal disorders, ischemic heart disease, stroke, lower respiratory infections, diarrheal diseases, road injuries, COPD, diabetes, congenital anomalies, back/neck pain.

**Global Health Priorities:**
- **Maternal/Child Health:** Reduce MMR (maternal mortality ratio - global target <70 per 100,000 live births by 2030). Under-5 mortality target <25 per 1000 live births. Skilled birth attendance. Postnatal care. Exclusive breastfeeding.
- **Infectious Disease Control:** HIV, TB, malaria elimination/control. Neglected tropical diseases (NTDs: schistosomiasis, onchocerciasis, lymphatic filariasis, trachoma, leprosy, Chagas, sleeping sickness, leishmaniasis, buruli ulcer, yaws, dracunculiasis). Helminth control (deworming). Emerging infectious disease threats (pandemic preparedness, One Health approach).
- **Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs):** 15x30 target (15% reduction in premature NCD mortality by 2030). Tobacco control (MPOWER). Salt reduction. Physical activity promotion. Alcohol control. Air pollution reduction. Cancer screening. Essential NCD medicines and technologies.
- **Universal Health Coverage (UHC):** All people receive quality health services without financial hardship. Primary health care approach. Essential package of services. Financial risk protection.
- **Mental Health:** Global burden large but underfunded. Mental health gap (mhGAP) program. Integration into primary care. Reducing stigma. Suicide prevention. Substance use treatment.
- **Climate Change & Health:** Direct effects (heat waves, extreme weather events). Indirect effects (vector-borne disease spread, food/water insecurity, air pollution from wildfires, displacement, mental health impacts). Health system resilience. Mitigation (reduce health sector emissions). Adaptation (early warning systems, climate-resilient infrastructure).

### 9.4 Vaccination Schedules

**WHO Recommended Routine Immunizations for Children:**
- **BCG:** Birth (or first contact) - TB prevention, primarily in high-burden countries. Not used in US (low prevalence).
- **HepB:** Birth, then with DTP-containing vaccines, or 3-4 doses schedule. Birth dose critical to prevent perinatal transmission.
- **Polio (OPV/IPV):** Multiple doses in first year, booster in second year. OPV (oral, live attenuated) used in endemic/polio-free transition countries. IPV (inactivated) used in polio-free countries. Global polio eradication initiative close to goal but wild polio still circulating in Afghanistan and Pakistan (cVDPV in several countries).
- **DTP (Diphtheria, Tetanus, Pertussis):** 3 primary doses (2, 4, 6 months), booster (15-18 mo, 4-6 yr, adolescent/adult Tdap). Acellular pertussis (aP) in many high-income countries.
- **Hib (H. influenzae type B):** 3 primary doses or 2 + booster. Part of pentavalent/hexavalent combination.
- **PCV (Pneumococcal conjugate):** 3 doses (2, 4, 6 mo or 2, 4 mo + booster at 12-15 mo) depending on schedule. PCV10 and PCV13 available. PCV15 and PCV20 newer.
- **Rotavirus:** 2 or 3 doses (2, 4 mo or 2, 4, 6 mo depending on vaccine). Oral, live attenuated.
- **Measles (MCV1, MCV2):** First dose at 9 or 12 months (high burden vs low burden). Second dose at 15-18 mo or 4-6 yr. MMR (measles, mumps, rubella) or MR. Measles elimination goal, but progress reversed in recent years (coverage gaps).
- **Rubella:** Combined with measles (MR/MMR). Prevent congenital rubella syndrome.
- **HPV:** 1 or 2 doses (9-14 yr). 3 doses if immunocompromised or starting later. Prevents cervical cancer, other HPV-related cancers.
- **Yellow Fever:** Single dose, life-long protection. Required for travel to endemic countries (International Certificate of Vaccination).
- **Typhoid:** Conjugate vaccine (single dose, children from 6 mo) or Vi polysaccharide (booster q3yr). School-age children in endemic areas.
- **Cholera:** Oral vaccine (2 doses). For outbreak response and endemic areas.
- **Meningococcal (A, C, W, Y):** In sub-Saharan Africa meningitis belt (MenAfriVac for group A).
- **COVID-19:** Multiple WHO EUL vaccines (mRNA, viral vector, inactivated, protein subunit). Primary series + boosters. Adapted for variants.

**Adult & At-Risk Group Vaccines:**
- **Influenza:** Annual, all adults. High-dose or adjuvanted for elderly.
- **Pneumococcal:** PCV15/PCV20 (all adults >=65, younger with risk factors). PPSV23 (if PCV13 given previously). Immunocompromised, CSF leak, cochlear implant receive special schedule.
- **Tdap/Td:** Every 10 years. Tdap once (preferably 11-12 yr, then Td). Tdap in every pregnancy (27-36 wk).
- **Shingles (Herpes Zoster):** Shingrix (recombinant, adjuvanted), 2 doses 2-6 months apart. >=50 yr, and immunocompromised >=19 yr.
- **HepA:** 2 doses (6-18 mo apart). Travelers, men who have sex with men, IDU, chronic liver disease, food handlers, outbreak settings.
- **HepB:** 3 doses (0, 1, 6 mo) or 2 doses (Heplisav-B). All adults aged 19-59 (ACIP universal recommendation), >59 with risk factors.
- **HPV:** Catch-up through age 26, shared decision through 45.
- **MMR:** 1-2 doses if born after 1957 and no immunity. Contraindicated in pregnancy, immunocompromised.
- **Varicella:** 2 doses if no evidence of immunity. Contraindicated in pregnancy, immunocompromised.
- **Meningococcal:** MenACWY for adolescents, college freshmen in dorms, military, asplenia, complement deficiency. MenB for adolescents/young adults (shared decision), asplenia, complement deficiency.
- **Rabies:** Pre-exposure prophylaxis (3 doses) for high-risk (veterinarians, lab workers, travelers to high-risk areas with limited access to PEP). Post-exposure prophylaxis: wound cleaning + RIG (rabies immune globulin) + vaccine (4 doses or 3 doses depending on schedule and prior vaccination). Immediate after exposure (rabies is 99.9% fatal once symptomatic).

### 9.5 Global Health Challenges and Initiatives

**Millennium Development Goals (MDGs, 2000-2015):** 8 goals focusing on poverty, hunger, education, gender equality, child mortality, maternal health, HIV/TB/malaria, environmental sustainability, global partnership. Significant progress, especially MDG 4 (child mortality reduction) and MDG 6 (disease control).

**Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs, 2015-2030):** 17 goals, 169 targets. SDG 3: Ensure healthy lives and promote well-being for all at all ages. Targets include UHC (3.8), maternal/child mortality (3.1, 3.2), NCDs (3.4), substance abuse (3.5), road injuries (3.6), reproductive health (3.7), infectious diseases (3.3), tobacco control (3.a), essential medicines (3.b), health financing (3.c), health workforce (3.c), early warning systems for global health risks (3.d). Health determinants integrated across all SDGs.

**Pandemic Preparedness:** COVID-19 exposed gaps in global health security. WHO Pandemic Treaty (under negotiation). Pandemic Fund at World Bank. Strengthen IHR core capacities. Early warning systems (surveillance for novel pathogens). Global vaccine manufacturing capacity. Pathogen access and benefit-sharing system. One Health approach (human-animal-environment interface, 75% of novel pathogens zoonotic). Strategic stockpiles (PPE, ventilators, vaccines, antivirals). Decentralized clinical trial networks.

**Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR):** Projected 10 million deaths/year by 2050 if unaddressed. WHO Global Action Plan on AMR. National action plans in most countries. Antibiotic stewardship (hospital and community). Infection prevention and control. Water/sanitation/hygiene. Access to quality, affordable antibiotics (while conserving through stewardship). New antibiotic development incentives (subscription/pull incentives, push incentives: CARB-X, GARDP). Surveillance (GLASS: Global Antimicrobial Resistance Surveillance System). One Health AMR surveillance.

**Neglected Tropical Diseases (NTDs):** WHO roadmap for NTDs 2021-2030. Targets: 90% reduction in need for interventions against NTDs, elimination of at least one NTD in 100 countries. Diseases: lymphatic filariasis (elephantiasis, mass drug administration DEC + albendazole), onchocerciasis (river blindness, ivermectin MDA), schistosomiasis (snail control, praziquantel MDA), soil-transmitted helminths (STH: Ascaris, Trichuris, hookworm, albendazole/mebendazole MDA in school-age children), trachoma (SAFE strategy: surgery for trichiasis, antibiotics [azithromycin], facial cleanliness, environmental improvement), leprosy (MDT: multidrug therapy, free since 1995), Chagas disease (vector control, benznidazole/nifurtimox), sleeping sickness/HAT (testse fly control, new oral fexinidazole), visceral leishmaniasis/Kala-azar (sandfly control, liposomal amphotericin B), dracunculiasis/guinea worm (water filtration, no vaccine/treatment, near eradication - only ~14 human cases in 2023), buruli ulcer, yaws, mycetoma, chromoblastomycosis, scabies, snakebite envenoming (recently added to NTD list, antivenom access).

### 9.6 Epidemiology of Key Global Diseases

**HIV/AIDS:** ~38 million living with HIV (2022). ~1.5 million new infections/year. ~630,000 deaths/year. Highest prevalence in Sub-Saharan Africa (Eastern/Southern Africa ~6.2% adult prevalence). 28 million on ART (76% coverage). UNAIDS 95-95-95 targets: 95% know status, 95% on ART, 95% virally suppressed (by 2030). Vertical transmission (PMTCT) rate <5% with adequate ART. Key populations at higher risk: men who have sex with men, sex workers, people who inject drugs, transgender individuals, prisoners.

**Tuberculosis:** ~10.6 million new cases (2022). ~1.3 million deaths (among HIV-negative) + 167,000 deaths among HIV-positive. ~450,000 MDR-TB cases. Highest burden: India (27%), Indonesia (10%), Philippines (7%), China (7%), Pakistan (6%), Nigeria (5%), Bangladesh (4%). WHO End TB Strategy: 90% reduction in deaths, 80% reduction in incidence by 2030 (vs 2015). Challenges: underdiagnosis, MDR-TB, HIV co-infection, funding gaps.

**Malaria:** ~249 million cases, ~608,000 deaths (2022). 95% of cases in Africa. Children <5 account for ~80% of deaths in Africa. P. falciparum predominant in Africa; P. vivax in Americas, SE Asia, Western Pacific. Insecticide resistance (pyrethroids) emerging. Partial artemisinin resistance in SE Asia (and recently in Africa). RTS,S/AS01 (Mosquirix) - first malaria vaccine, WHO recommended for children in moderate-high transmission settings. R21/Matrix-M vaccine also now WHO prequalified. Mass distribution of ITNs, indoor residual spraying, seasonal malaria chemoprevention (SMC) with sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine + amodiaquine for children under 5 in Sahel. Goal: 90% reduction in incidence and mortality by 2030.

**Neglected Tropical Diseases (NTDs) Burden:** Affect >1 billion people, primarily in tropical/subtropical regions. Cause significant morbidity (blindness, disfigurement, disability, cognitive impairment, growth faltering, impaired pregnancy outcomes). Mass drug administration (MDA) programs deliver preventive chemotherapy to hundreds of millions annually. Integration with WASH and vector control important.

**Maternal and Child Health:** Global MMR ~223 per 100,000 live births (2020) - down 34% from 2000 but far from SDG target <70. Sub-Saharan Africa MMR ~536 (~70% of global maternal deaths). Leading causes: hemorrhage (27%), hypertensive disorders (14%), sepsis (11%), abortion complications (8%), embolism (3%), other direct/indirect causes. Under-5 mortality rate: 37 per 1000 live births (2022). Leading causes: preterm birth complications, pneumonia, birth asphyxia/trauma, congenital anomalies, diarrhea, malaria, neonatal sepsis. Neonatal deaths ~47% of all under-5 deaths. Skilled birth attendance globally ~86% (lowest in Sub-Saharan Africa ~70%). Postnatal care essential.

**Nutrition:** Stunting (low height-for-age) affects ~148 million under-5 children (22%) globally. Wasting (low weight-for-height) affects ~45 million. Micronutrient deficiencies: iron (anemia affects ~30% of women 15-49, 40% of under-5s globally), vitamin A (leading cause of preventable blindness, increases infection risk), iodine (mental impairment, goiter), zinc (growth, diarrhea/infection risk, oral rehydration + zinc for diarrhea management). Exclusive breastfeeding for first 6 months recommended (coverage ~44% globally). Double/triple burden: undernutrition, overweight/obesity, micronutrient deficiencies coexist in many countries.

**Non-Communicable Diseases (NCDs):** ~74% of all deaths globally (41 million annually). Cardiovascular diseases (17.9 million), cancers (9.3 million), chronic respiratory diseases (4.1 million), diabetes (1.5 million direct, 2.0 million with kidney). Tobacco use kills ~8 million/year. Harmful alcohol use kills ~3 million/year. UN High-Level Meetings on NCDs. WHO Best Buys (cost-effective interventions): tobacco taxation, reduction of salt/sugar/trans fats in food, promotion of physical activity, reduction of harmful alcohol use, cardiovascular disease prevention and management (including hypertension and diabetes), cancer screening/vaccination (cervical), essential NCD medicines and technologies.

### 9.7 Travel Medicine

**Pre-Travel Consultation:** Assess itinerary, duration, activities, accommodations, medical history, vaccination status. Immunizations: routine (MMR, DTaP, polio, influenza, COVID-19), required (yellow fever proof for travel to/from endemic countries, Saudi Arabia [Hajj/Umrah: MenACWY, polio]), recommended (hepA, typhoid, rabies, cholera, Japanese encephalitis, tick-borne encephalitis, hepB, meningococcal, cholera/ETEC). Malaria prophylaxis (consider resistance pattern, itinerary, duration, patient factors: atovaquone-proguanil, doxycycline, mefloquine, chloroquine [limited due to resistance], tafenoquine [single dose for short trips, G6PD testing required]). Altitude illness prevention (acetazolamide). Travelers diarrhea prevention (food/water precautions) and self-treatment (azithromycin, rifaximin, ciprofloxacin [caution with resistance], loperamide for symptom control). Motion sickness (scopolamine patch, dimenhydrinate, meclizine). Jet lag management (melatonin, light exposure, schedule adjustment). Insect precautions (DEET, permethrin-treated clothing, bed nets, long sleeves/pants). Sun protection. Travel health insurance. Medical evacuation coverage. First aid kit.

**Post-Travel Evaluation:** Fever in returned traveler: malaria (thick/thin blood smears, RDT), dengue (NS1, IgM, PCR), typhoid (blood/stool cultures, Widal), rickettsial disease (scrub typhus, murine typhus, African tick bite fever - serology/doxycycline empirically), leptospirosis, chikungunya, Zika, COVID-19, influenza. Diarrhea: bacterial (bloody: Shigella, Campylobacter, E. coli, Salmonella), parasitic (Giardia, Entamoeba histolytica, Cryptosporidium, Cyclospora). Eosinophilia: consider helminth infection (schistosomiasis, strongyloidiasis, hookworm, filariasis). Chronic cough: TB (if prolonged exposure, endemic area). Screening: strongyloides serology before immunosuppression. G6PD deficiency common in malaria-endemic areas, affects drug choice.

### 9.8 Ethics in Global Health

**Key Principles:** Respect for autonomy (informed consent, community engagement), Beneficence (do good, maximize benefit), Non-maleficence (do no harm, minimize unintended consequences), Justice (fair distribution of research benefits/burdens, equity, avoid exploitation of vulnerable populations). Declaration of Helsinki for research. CIOMS guidelines. Framework for ethical conduct of outbreak research (emergency use of unproven interventions). Equitable vaccine distribution (COVAX mechanism during COVID-19 - highlighted global inequity). Community engagement essential in global health research and programs.

### 9.9 Health Systems

**Building Blocks (WHO Framework):** Service delivery (quality, safety, access), Health workforce (adequate numbers, skill mix, distribution), Health information systems (surveillance, HMIS, vital registration, electronic health records), Access to essential medicines (supply chain, quality assurance, rational use), Financing (revenue collection, pooling, purchasing - health insurance models: social health insurance, national health service, private insurance, community-based insurance), Leadership/governance (policy, regulation, accountability, transparency). Primary health care approach essential for UHC. Strong referral systems. Community health workers (CHWs) key especially in low-resource settings.

**Health Financing:** Out-of-pocket payments (direct payments at point of service - most regressive, creates catastrophic health expenditure, impoverishment). Prepayment mechanisms: general government revenue (tax-based), social health insurance (payroll contributions), private health insurance. Strategic purchasing (buying services based on population needs and performance, not just paying for inputs). Results-based financing. Provider payment mechanisms (salary, capitation, fee-for-service, diagnosis-related groups, global budget). Universal health coverage goal: all people receive quality needed services without financial hardship. WHO UHC index measures coverage of essential services.
